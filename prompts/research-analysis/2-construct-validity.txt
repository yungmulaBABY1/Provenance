CONSTRUCT-VALIDITY AUDIT PROMPT:

CLAIM VS. MEASUREMENT

Purpose:

Use this prompt to audit one paper, study, report, policy document, or institutional claim before making any broad claim about a field.

Do not evaluate whether the authors are good people.

Do not infer private motive.

Do not summarize the source politely.

Do not defer to prestige, peer review, journal status, citation count, institutional authority, or the social acceptability of the conclusion.

Your task is narrower:

Compare what the source actually measured to what the source claims, and to what later citations, institutions, or public arguments use it to prove.

The point is not to decide whether the field is good or bad.

The point is to test whether the measurement supports the claim.

COMPACT VOCABULARY
Full rules: shared_primitives.txt

VERIFICATION STATE â how thoroughly a source has been checked. This is separate
from evidence type. A peer-reviewed study can be strong in evidence type while
remaining weakly verified.

- LOCATED: source exists and metadata is confirmed; content is unverified.
- ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED: abstract supports relevance; methods and results are not
  verified.
- FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED: full text was read and directly supports the specific claim.
  Do not assign this merely because selected excerpts were supplied.
- RECEPTION-CONFIRMED: replies, replications, corrections, retractions, or citation
  contexts were checked against retrieved sources.
- CONTESTED: credible disagreement exists; who is correct remains unresolved.
- REBUTTED: later work directly and substantially challenges the source and the
  challenge was not evidently answered.
- PROVISIONAL: access or retrieval is insufficient, including unverified model
  memory or unresolved source leads.

Rules:

1. Never report a verification state above what was actually checked.
2. Evidence type and verification state are separate axes; report both.
3. FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED and RECEPTION-CONFIRMED may both apply.
4. Apply the ladder symmetrically to sources supporting and opposing the claim.
5. A PROVISIONAL source may not anchor a MAJOR or FATAL finding.

FAILURE LOCATION â where the failure actually lives:

- PAPER: the source itself overclaims or uses an invalid proxy.
- CITATION: the source is defensible but later literature inflates it.
- PR / MEDIA: a press release or coverage strips caveats or broadens the claim.
- MODEL / RETRIEVAL: summarizers or search systems reproduce the inflated version.
- MIXED: more than one layer contributes; specify which layer contributes what.

Attacking a defensible paper for its press release is a category error. Classify
the responsible layer honestly.

5. CONFOUND AUDIT

- What confounds would a practitioner, clinician, performer, or domain expert immediately notice?

- Did the authors acknowledge them?
- Did later citations forget the confounds?
- Would the omitted confounds change the interpretation?

6. ECOLOGICAL VALIDITY CHECK

- Does the sample resemble the population the claim is applied to?
- Does the time scale match the real process?
- Does the measured behavior occur under realistic pressure, incentives, constraints, or selection?

- Moderate ecological validity
- Low ecological validity

13. THE CONCEDE TEST

Run this on every proposed finding before rating severity or choosing the verdict.

For each finding, ask:

IF THIS FINDING WERE FULLY CONCEDED â granted entirely, with no argument â WOULD
THE CLAIM UNDER AUDIT STILL STAND?

- SURVIVES: the claim still holds. This is a limitation, not a defeater. Report
  it, but it may not be rated MAJOR or FATAL and may not anchor the verdict.
- NARROWS: the claim survives only in smaller scope, such as a different
  population, weaker effect, or narrower conditions. State the surviving claim
  explicitly.
- DEFEATS: the audited claim does not follow. This is load-bearing.

Create a table:

| Finding | Concede test: SURVIVES / NARROWS / DEFEATS | Surviving claim if NARROWS |
|---|---|---|

This test separates limitations from claim-defeating failures without softening
either.

It cuts both ways:

- A DEFEATS result strengthens a finding by showing it is load-bearing.
- A SURVIVES result shows that the finding cannot carry the verdict.
- Do not turn a raw count of overlapping limitations into a claim-defeating
  verdict. Consolidate duplicated findings and judge what scope the claim actually
  retains.

SCOPE-LOSS ASSESSMENT â REQUIRED FOR EVERY NARROWS RESULT

Do not map verdicts by counting NARROWS findings. State how much of the audited
claim survives after the finding is conceded:

- INCIDENTAL NARROWING: wording, boundary conditions, or precision changes, but
  the claim remains substantially the same in population, construct, direction,
  and practical use. This ordinarily supports verdict B, not C.
- MATERIAL NARROWING: the claim survives only for a meaningfully smaller
  population, weaker construct, shorter time horizon, reduced effect, or more
  limited application. The strong claim is invalid, but a useful narrower claim
  remains. This supports verdict C.
- NEAR-DEFEAT NARROWING: only a trivial, tautological, or practically different
  remainder survives. Do not use NARROWS to avoid DEFEATS. Reclassify as DEFEATS
  if the surviving proposition no longer does the work for which the audited claim
  is being used.

For each NARROWS result, add:

| Finding | Scope-loss level | Original claim | Surviving claim | What practical or inferential work is lost? |
|---|---|---|---|---|

VERDICT MAPPING:

- No DEFEATS and no MATERIAL NARROWING: use A or B according to the remaining
  limitations and evidentiary strength.
- One or more MATERIAL NARROWING findings and no DEFEATS: use C.
- One or more DEFEATS: use D, E, or F according to the mechanism, severity, and
  failure location.
- If evidence is insufficient to run the test reliably: use G.

The decisive question is not how many findings narrow the claim. It is whether
the surviving claim remains substantially the claim named in map field 6 and can
still support the use under audit.

Do not use the concede test to cut findings merely because they are limitations.
Report them, but enforce the severity cap.
