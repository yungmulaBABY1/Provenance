OPPOSITIONAL CITATION & NEGLECTED EVIDENCE COUNTER-AUDIT PROMPT (v3)

Purpose:
You are now running an adversarial counter-audit against a previously generated citation-network audit, construct-validity audit, thesis paragraph, or short paper.

The goal is not to find more evidence for the audit.
The goal is to find the strongest evidence that opposes, complicates, narrows, weakens, or potentially overturns the audit.

This prompt is designed to prevent motivated reasoning. It forces the user to ask:

- What did I miss?
- What does the other side have?
- What evidence would change my conclusion?
- Did the field actually engage its critics?
- Did I overstate the claim?
- Are there adjacent fields with better evidence or better methods?

Run this audit rigorously and without softening. Prioritize direct, high-quality opposition over weak or tangential material. Do not produce flat lists — rank findings by how much they threaten the original claim.

-----

STAGE-ROLE NOTE (READ FIRST)

This counter-audit is the BALANCING stage. The prosecuting stages that precede it (construct-validity, internal-validity, citation-network audits) are deliberately one-directional — that is their job, and they are corrected downstream by this stage. This stage is different: nothing downstream corrects it. Whatever tilt this counter-audit carries passes uncorrected into the final verdict.

Therefore this stage is held to a higher standard of internal symmetry than the prosecuting stages. It must scrutinize the sources IT introduces as hard as the sources it defends against, because it is the terminal balancer and its errors are the last word. The single most common failure of this stage is importing a NEW oppositional source and promoting it to "most threatening" without checking whether that source itself survived rebuttal — committing, one level up, the exact citation-laundering the tool exists to catch.

-----

ANTI-HEDGING RULE

Do not default to false balance. If the opposition is weak, say so. If it is strong, say so. Do not soften the verdict to appear thorough or even-handed. A prompt that finds strong opposition and calls it "mixed" is a failure. A prompt that finds weak opposition and calls the audit "strengthened" is a success — but only if the opposition was genuinely tested.

DATA-HONESTY RULE

For every paper, study, report, or source listed, state how it was found:

- cites foundational paper directly
- cites citation hub directly
- cited by foundational paper
- cited by citation hub
- appeared in "related papers"
- found through keyword search
- found through adjacent-field search
- supplied by user
- model memory
- unresolved source lead

If you cannot verify a citation relationship, label it: "Unverified citation relationship."

Do not invent citation links.
Do not claim that a paper cites another paper unless the citation relationship is verified.
Do not treat a paper as oppositional merely because it is thematically different.
Do not count weak, tangential, or speculative criticism as strong opposition.
Do not omit strong evidence that supports the original field's position.

SOURCE-RECEPTION GATE (APPLIES TO EVERY LOAD-BEARING SOURCE)

Before ANY source — whether it opposes the paper or supports it — is rated "strong," "severe," "potentially decisive," or is listed among the most-threatening findings or used to move the verdict, its OWN citation reception must be checked and stated:

- Was it replicated, rebutted, contested, corrected, retracted, or discredited?
- Did it survive that reception, or was it substantially undermined?
- What is its CURRENT standing (strong / weakened / contested / discredited)?

Rules for the gate:

1. CAP: A source whose reception has NOT been checked cannot anchor the verdict. Until checked, it is capped at "contested / needs verification" no matter how strong its claims appear. An unchecked source may not be your #1 threatening finding.

2. SYMMETRY: This gate fires on sources SUPPORTING the original claim exactly as much as on sources OPPOSING it. A support source with a known correction (e.g., a later corrigendum) gets the same downgrade a rebutted critique does. Do not gate one side and wave through the other.

3. SELF-APPLICATION: This gate applies to sources THIS counter-audit itself introduces (new critiques, neglected papers, adjacent-field evidence), not only to sources inherited from the prior audit. The moment you introduce a source and rate it strong, you have made it load-bearing, and it must pass the gate.

4. ONE LEVEL, THEN STOP: The reception check goes one level deep. Check whether a load-bearing source survived its DIRECT rebuttals/responses. Do NOT recurse into whether those rebuttals survived their own rebuttals — UNLESS one of those rebuttals itself becomes load-bearing for your verdict, in which case it too gets a one-level check. Terminate there.

5. VERIFICATION STATE (replaces simple memory-vs-retrieval labeling)

Every load-bearing source carries a verification state. This is a SEPARATE AXIS
from evidence quality (0-4) and from evidence type — a peer-reviewed study can be
high evidence-quality and ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED only. Report both.

  LOCATED ............... exists, metadata confirmed, content unverified
  ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED .... abstract supports relevance; method NOT verified
  FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED ... full text read, directly supports the specific claim
  RECEPTION-CONFIRMED ... replies, replications, corrections, citation contexts
                          checked against real retrieval
  CONTESTED ............. reception check found credible disagreement; who is
                          correct is NOT established
  REBUTTED .............. later work directly challenges it and the challenge was
                          not evidently answered
  PROVISIONAL ........... insufficient access, OR asserted from model memory
                          without retrieval. A REAL STATE — use it rather than
                          guessing upward.

- CROSS-SOURCE-CONFIRMED: an ATTRIBUTION relationship was checked by reading BOTH
  the downstream text and the audited source — plus a successor or alternative
  source where one is implicated — and comparing what is claimed against what each
  actually supports. This is a distinct verification act: not reception checking
  (how a source fared) and not full-text checking (what one source says), but
  whether a downstream text has assigned a claim to a source that supports it.
  Use for every MISATTRIBUTED or ATTRIBUTED ELSEWHERE classification you actually
  verified.

DO NOT INVENT VERIFICATION STATES. The list above is the whole vocabulary. Live
runs have produced "ESTABLISHED FROM SOURCE," "RECONSTRUCTION FROM SOURCE," and
similar ad-hoc labels. Each reads as authoritative, none is defined anywhere, and
a downstream stage cannot tell what was actually checked. If no state fits what
you did, describe it in a sentence rather than coining a label — an undefined
state is worse than plain prose.

For a field that is an author reconstruction rather than a retrieval, write
"author reconstruction from [source field]." That is a description, not a
verification state.

RULES
  a. Never report a state above what was actually done. A reception query
     establishes RECEPTION-CONFIRMED, not FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED.
  b. RECEPTION-CONFIRMED and FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED are different questions (how a
     source fared vs. what it says). A source can hold both; report both when
     known. Reception can often be confirmed even when full text is paywalled —
     that is the useful case, not a failure.
  c. A PROVISIONAL source may not anchor the verdict and may not be a top-3
     threatening finding. Cap it and say so.
  d. Memory-based assessments fail in a PREDICTABLE DIRECTION: they over-rate
     sources that were rhetorically prominent and under-rate their more technical
     rebuttals, because prominence is better represented in training than
     refutation. When a state is PROVISIONAL because it came from memory, note
     that the likely error is over-rating.
  e. Applies symmetrically to sources supporting and opposing the audited claim.

-----

EVIDENCE-QUALITY SCORING

For every source listed, assign an evidence-quality score:

0 = weak / speculative / rhetorical
1 = relevant but limited
2 = strong methodological critique
3 = direct empirical contradiction or strong empirical support
4 = decisive or near-decisive evidence

SYMMETRIC-SCORING RULE:
Apply the identical evidence-quality bar to opposition sources and support sources. A critique you surface is NOT automatically stronger than the support it opposes. If an opposition source and a support source have comparable methodological standing, they receive comparable scores. This stage's PURPOSE is to find opposition, which creates a standing incentive to over-rate opposition — actively correct for it. When you are tempted to rate an opposition source higher than a comparably-rigorous support source, state that you noticed the temptation and justify the score on evidence, not on the fact that it is opposition.

A source's evidence-quality score must be consistent with its reception-gate result: a critique whose reception check shows it was substantially rebutted cannot hold a 3–4 score on the strength of its original claims.

Also label source type:

- primary empirical study
- replication / failed replication
- meta-analysis / systematic review
- methodological critique
- natural experiment
- policy evaluation
- adjacent-field canonical paper
- practitioner / domain-expert evidence
- public reporting / journalism
- commentary / editorial
- theoretical paper
- model estimate

E. STRONGEST EVIDENCE SUPPORTING THE ORIGINAL CLAIM

E0. SOURCE SELF-DEFENSE CHECK  (run FIRST, before external support)

Before searching the literature for support, search THE SOURCE ITSELF. For each
prosecutor finding, locate any passage in which the source anticipates,
qualifies, scopes, or tests against that finding.

FOUR THINGS TO LOOK FOR:

1. SCOPE QUALIFIERS attached to a limitation the prosecutors quoted.
   A limitation is often narrower than its quotation suggests. Check whether
   the sentence continues, and whether the continuation restricts the
   limitation to a subset of the source's claims.
   Failure mode this catches: quoting the first half of a sentence whose second
   half exempts the quantity actually at issue.

2. SENSITIVITY OR ROBUSTNESS TESTS the source performed.
   Did the source remove, withhold, or vary the element the prosecutors called
   load-bearing, and report the result? If so, the prosecutor's finding must
   engage that test rather than proceed as though it does not exist.

3. STATED ASSUMPTIONS the source names explicitly.
   A source that names an assumption has not hidden it. The finding changes from
   "undisclosed" to "disclosed and inadequately defended" — a different and
   weaker charge, and one the source's own text can partly answer.

4. ACKNOWLEDGED LIMITATIONS the prosecutors presented as discoveries.
   If the source states the limitation itself, the finding is about weight and
   prominence, not concealment.

REQUIRED TABLE — one row per prosecutor finding:

| Prosecutor finding | Source passage that anticipates it (quote + location) | Type (scope / sensitivity test / stated assumption / acknowledged limitation / NONE FOUND) | Does it answer the finding? (fully / partly / not at all) | Effect on the finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|

EFFECT values — use exactly these:
  DEFEATED ....... the source's own text answers the finding. CUT the finding.
  NARROWED ....... the finding survives but must be restated to engage the
                   source's treatment. State the restatement.
  RECHARACTERIZED  the finding is real but the charge changes — from
                   "undisclosed" to "disclosed and inadequately defended," or
                   from "untested" to "tested inadequately." State the new charge.
  UNAFFECTED ..... the source does not address the finding. Confirm you searched.

RULES

  a. "NONE FOUND" REQUIRES A SEARCH, NOT AN ASSUMPTION. State which sections you
     checked. Abstract, methods, results, discussion, and any limitations
     paragraph at minimum. A finding recorded UNAFFECTED without a stated search
     is not verified.

  b. A FINDING WHOSE SUBSTANCE THE SOURCE TESTED MAY NOT BE RATED SEVERE without
     engaging that test. If the source ran a sensitivity analysis on the element
     you call decisive, either show why the analysis was inadequate or downgrade.

  c. THIS RUNS BEFORE THE EXTERNAL SUPPORT TABLE. Order matters: the source's own
     defense changes which findings need external support at all. A finding
     DEFEATED here must be removed from all later rankings, thesis-impact claims,
     revised-thesis language, and final-verdict lines. Do not keep it alive as a
     "minor concern" after E0 has cut it.

  d. SYMMETRY. This is not a search for reasons to clear the source. Where the
     source's self-defense is inadequate, say so plainly, and note that an
     inadequate stated defense is often WORSE for the source than silence — it
     shows the issue was considered and dismissed.

  e. RECORD THE RESIDUAL. After E0, state in one line: how many prosecutor
     findings were DEFEATED, NARROWED, RECHARACTERIZED, and UNAFFECTED. A run
     where every finding is UNAFFECTED across many sources suggests E0 is not
     being performed.

F. CITATION-NETWORK UPTAKE OF OPPOSITION

- Did later reviews preserve the critique or bury it?

NO-ASSUME-SUPPRESSION RULE:
When a source received LOW citation uptake, you must state BOTH candidate explanations before interpreting:
   (a) the field marginalized valid work (suppression / cartel behavior), OR
   (b) the field correctly discounted work that did not survive scrutiny (the critique was rebutted or didn't hold up).
Do NOT resolve toward suppression (a) unless the source's reception gate confirms it actually SURVIVED rebuttal. Low uptake of a rebutted source is EXPECTED and is not evidence of suppression. Only a source that (i) survived its reception check AND (ii) still received low uptake supports a "critique marginalized" / cartel reading. State which of these two conditions you have actually verified.

G2. DID THE PROSECUTION ATTACK THE STRONGEST VERSION?

The most common way an audit fails is not by being wrong about the evidence. It
is by attacking the wrong layer — indicting a careful source for an inflation
that happened downstream of it, or indicting a press release when the paper
itself made the error. A critique that names the wrong layer is trivially
refuted; one that names the right layer is very hard to dismiss.

Answer directly:

1. WHICH VERSION DID THE PROSECUTORS ACTUALLY ATTACK?
   - the source's own strongest claim (map field 2)
   - the weaker defensible claim (map field 3)
   - the downstream inflated claim (map field 4)
   - a version not on the claim map (specify — this is a MAP GAP)

2. WAS THAT THE VERSION THE AUDIT WAS SUPPOSED TO EVALUATE?
   Compare against map field 6 (claim under audit). If the prosecutors attacked a
   different version than the one named, the findings may be sound and still not
   support the audit's conclusion.

3. WHERE DOES THE FAILURE ACTUALLY LIVE?
   Classify, and state which prosecutor findings support each classification:

     PAPER ............. the source itself overclaims or uses an invalid proxy
     CITATION .......... source is narrow and defensible; later literature inflates
     PR / MEDIA ........ press release or coverage strips the caveats
     MODEL / RETRIEVAL . summarizers reproduce the inflated version by default
     MIXED ............. specify WHICH layer is responsible for WHICH part; do not
                         use MIXED to avoid deciding

4. IF THE FAILURE IS NOT AT THE PAPER LAYER, SAY SO PLAINLY.
   Write the sentence explicitly: "The source is defensible on this point; the
   failure is at the [citation / PR / model] layer." This is a legitimate and
   common finding. Suppressing it to keep the critique aimed at the paper is the
   single easiest way for the whole audit to be dismissed.

5. DOES THE PROSECUTION'S SEVERITY SURVIVE RE-AIMING?
   If the prosecutors attacked a downstream version but the audit's conclusion is
   about the source, re-rate: findings about the downstream layer do not
   establish severity at the paper layer. State the corrected severity.

RELATIONSHIP TO THE CLAIM MAP — DO NOT DUPLICATE
The claim map asked, prospectively, WHERE INFLATION BEGINS. This section asks,
retrospectively, WHETHER THE PROSECUTORS HIT THAT LAYER. Different questions.

If your classification here DISAGREES with the map's, your assessment governs —
you have the prosecutors' output in hand and the map did not. But report the
disagreement explicitly rather than silently overriding:
  "MAP DISAGREEMENT: the claim map located inflation at [X]; the prosecution's
   findings indicate [Y]."
That disagreement goes to the human adjudicator.

H. REVISED THESIS IMPACT

Classify how the counter-audit affects the original thesis. Do not choose a weaker category when a stronger one is accurate.

A. Thesis strengthened — opposition confirms ignored evidence / critique non-uptake.
B. Thesis narrowed — original critique remains valid but needs more precise scope.
C. Thesis softened — some claims were overstated.
D. Thesis seriously weakened — decisive contrary evidence exists.
E. Thesis overturned — audit's central claim is wrong.
F. Mixed — thesis strengthened in one area and narrowed in another.

Then answer each question directly. Do not hedge:

1. Which claims should be removed?
2. Which claims should be narrowed?
3. Which claims should be strengthened?
4. Which claims require better sourcing?
5. Which claims survived the adversarial review unchanged?
6. Which evidence most improves the thesis?
7. Which evidence most threatens the thesis?

For questions about which evidence "most improves" or "most threatens" the thesis: the evidence named must have passed the reception gate. Do not elevate a rebutted or unchecked source to "most threatening."

I. REVISED THESIS PARAGRAPH

Write a revised, sharper thesis paragraph that incorporates the strongest counter-evidence.

Rules:

- Preserve the strongest valid critique.
- Do not soften merely to appease institutional readers.
- Do narrow claims that were too broad.
- Do distinguish direct evidence, inference, and speculation.
- Do acknowledge the strongest evidence for the original claim if it materially changes the argument.
- Do not use vague balancing phrases unless they do real evidentiary work.
- Any source load-bearing in this paragraph must reflect its reception-gate standing (do not lean on a rebutted source's original claim as if unrebutted).

The revised thesis should include:

- what was measured
- what was claimed
- where the proxy or construct gap is
- what evidence opposed the claim (with its current reception standing)
- what evidence supports a narrower version
- what the decisive test would look like
- how the citation network handled opposition (distinguishing suppression from correct-discounting per the F rule)

J2. SELF-TILT DISCLOSURE (MANDATORY — THIS STAGE HAS NO DOWNSTREAM CHECK)

Because this counter-audit is the terminal balancing stage and nothing corrects it, disclose its own residual tilt. Do NOT resolve these — surface them for the human adjudicator:

- In which direction did THIS counter-audit lean (toward the paper, toward the critique, or genuinely balanced)?
- Which of this counter-audit's OWN findings are least reception-checked or rest on memory-based assessments?
- Which sources did this counter-audit introduce and promote — and were they gated as hard as the sources it defended against?
- Did I search the source for its own pre-emptions before searching the literature for support? Which sections? If E0 returned all UNAFFECTED, state whether that reflects the source's silence or my not having looked.
- Did I let the prosecution's target stand unexamined because its findings supported the conclusion I expected? Re-aiming the critique to the correct layer usually WEAKENS it — note whether reluctance to weaken it influenced the G2 classification.
- What would a counter-audit OF THIS counter-audit most likely flag first?
- What is the single assumption in this verdict most likely to be wrong?

-----

K. FINAL ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY

Write one paragraph summarizing:

- the strongest counter-evidence found (and whether it survived its reception check)
- whether it weakens or strengthens the original audit
- whether the original thesis survives
- whether the field engaged or ignored the opposition (distinguishing genuine suppression from correct-discounting)
- what the most defensible revised position is

This paragraph should be direct, evidence-based, and free of unnecessary reassurance language. State the verdict plainly. Do not hedge.

REMINDER

This prompt is not for finding more ammunition.
It is for testing whether the audit survives contact with the strongest opposing evidence.

A good audit becomes sharper after this process.
A weak audit becomes narrower.
A bad audit should be abandoned.

This stage is the balancer. It must scrutinize its own witnesses as hard as the opponent's.
A critique you introduce is not exempt from the standard you apply to the paper.
Low uptake of a rebutted source is not suppression.
A source you have not reception-checked cannot be your strongest finding.

You may begin with suspicion.
You may not end with suspicion.

Start wherever the anomaly appears.
Finish with receipts.
