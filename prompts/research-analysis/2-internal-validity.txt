INTERNAL VALIDITY & REPLICATION AUDIT PROMPT

Purpose: This prompt audits the internal validity of a specific study or finding  -  whether
the result itself is likely to be real, replicable, and free of analytic artifacts  -  before
assessing what it claims or how it has been cited.

Citation laundering and proxy problems operate on the output of a study. P-hacking, HARKing
(Hypothesizing After Results are Known), selective reporting, and underpowered designs corrupt
the finding at the source. A finding that fails internal validity cannot support the claims
built on it regardless of how many papers cite it.

This prompt also covers AI-assisted data analysis. The failure mode is identical: a tool
produces a confident output, the user lacks the domain knowledge to audit whether the output
measures what the claim requires, and the output gets treated as a finding. Whether the tool
is a regression model, a citation network, or an LLM doing exploratory analysis  -  the proxy
failure mechanism is the same. The data generation audit below applies to both human-conducted
and AI-assisted analysis.

Run this audit before or alongside the construct validity audit. If the foundational finding
fails internal validity, note this in the thesis. The claim is doubly weak: the study may not
measure what it claims, and the finding may not be real.

COMPACT VOCABULARY Full rules: shared_primitives.txt

VERIFICATION STATE  -  how thoroughly a source has been checked. This is separate from
evidence type. A peer-reviewed study can be strong in evidence type while remaining weakly
verified.

- LOCATED: source exists and metadata is confirmed; content is unverified.
- ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED: abstract supports relevance; methods and results are not verified.
- FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED: full text was read and directly supports the specific claim. Do not
  assign this merely because selected excerpts were supplied.
- RECEPTION-CONFIRMED: replies, replications, corrections, retractions, or citation contexts
  were checked against retrieved sources.
- CONTESTED: credible disagreement exists; who is correct remains unresolved.
- REBUTTED: later work directly and substantially challenges the source and the challenge was
  not evidently answered.
- PROVISIONAL: access or retrieval is insufficient, including unverified model memory or
  unresolved source leads.

Rules:

1. Never report a verification state above what was actually checked.
2. Evidence type and verification state are separate axes; report both.
3. FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED and RECEPTION-CONFIRMED may both apply.
4. Apply the ladder symmetrically to sources supporting and opposing the claim.
5. A PROVISIONAL source may not anchor a MAJOR or FATAL finding.

FAILURE LOCATION  -  where the failure actually lives:

- PAPER: the source itself overclaims or its design/analysis cannot support the finding.
- CITATION: the source is defensible but later literature inflates it.
- PR / MEDIA: a press release or coverage strips caveats or broadens the claim.
- MODEL / RETRIEVAL: summarizers or search systems reproduce the inflated version.
- MIXED: more than one layer contributes; specify which layer contributes what.

Attacking a defensible paper for its press release is a category error. Classify the
responsible layer honestly.

DATA-HONESTY RULE

For every claim about study design, analysis choices, or replication status, state how it was
determined:

- reported in the paper itself
- reported in supplementary materials
- found in pre-registration record (OSF, ClinicalTrials, AsPredicted, etc.)
- found in replication literature
- found in methodological critique
- PROVISIONAL  -  model memory not checked against retrieval
- unknown / not reported

RECEPTION-MODULE CONFIRMATION RULE  -  REPLICATION AND POST-PUBLICATION CLAIMS

Sections F (Replication Status) and G (Data and Code Availability, reanalysis, PubPeer,
corrections) make claims about how a finding fared AFTER publication. These are reception
claims. Confirm them against retrieved sources rather than asserting them from model memory.

Use the citation-reception module (OpenAlex + Semantic Scholar), together with direct source
retrieval where available, to confirm:

- whether replication or failed-replication papers actually exist and how many;
- whether citation contexts contain replication-outcome language such as "failed to
  replicate," "we replicate," or "the effect did not hold";
- whether formal replies, comments, corrigenda, retractions, corrections, or expressions of
  concern appear in the citing/reply set.

Apply the compact verification vocabulary:

- Replication existence and tenor confirmed through retrieval: RECEPTION-CONFIRMED.
- Replication recalled or estimated but not retrieved: PROVISIONAL.
- Replication full text read and the technical result checked: FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED, and also
  RECEPTION-CONFIRMED where its relation to the foundational source was verified.

Label every replication-status and post-publication claim with its verification state. A
verdict may not be driven to E (Invalid) by a failed replication that is only PROVISIONAL.
Confirm that the failed replication exists before allowing it to anchor the verdict.

SCOPE FENCE: the module can confirm the EXISTENCE and reported TENOR of replications and
responses. It does not by itself adjudicate the technical outcome of a disputed replication
exchange. Determining who is right requires the relevant full texts and remains PROVISIONAL or
CONTESTED until they are examined.

Do not infer pre-registration compliance from the absence of information. If pre-registration
status is unknown, state it clearly.

SECTION A: DATA GENERATION AUDIT

Run this section before any analysis audit. A finding built on flawed data cannot be rescued
by clean analysis. AI-assisted analysis on flawed data produces confident wrong answers faster
than any previous tool.

1. How was this data collected?
- Who collected it, when, and under what conditions?
- Was collection prospective or retrospective?
- Was it observational, experimental, or administrative?
- Does the analysis treat it accordingly  -  or does it make causal claims from observational
  data?
2. What selection effects shaped the dataset?
- Who or what is included in the data?
- Who or what is systematically missing  -  and why?
- Is the sample representative of the population the claim is made about?
- What self-selection, survival, or survivorship bias might be present?
3. What does missingness look like?
- How much data is missing?
- Is missingness random (MCAR), related to observed variables (MAR), or related to unobserved
  variables (MNAR)?
- If MNAR: does the missingness pattern systematically bias the finding in one direction?
- How was missingness handled in the analysis  -  and was that handling appropriate?
4. What incentive structure governed data collection?
- Who benefits from particular findings emerging from this data?
- Was the data collected by the same party that stands to benefit from the result?
- Are there known cases where similar data collection incentives produced distorted findings?
- For administrative or platform data: what organizational decisions shaped what gets recorded
  versus what gets dropped?
5. For AI-assisted analysis specifically:
- Was the dataset inspected before analysis or fed directly to the model?
- Did the model flag data quality issues or proceed without surfacing them?
- Were the analysis outputs checked against the raw data or accepted as outputs?
- Was the adversarial check run: "What is the strongest methodological critique of this
  analysis? What does this measure that I might be mistaking for what I want it to measure?
  What would falsify this finding?"

|Data source|Collection method|Selection bias risk|Missingness pattern|Incentive structure risk|AI-analysis check run|
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|-------------------|------------------------|---------------------|

Risk options: Low / Moderate / High / Cannot assess AI-analysis check: Yes / No / Partial /
N/A

SECTION B: PRE-REGISTRATION AND ANALYSIS PLAN

1. Was the study pre-registered before data collection began?
- If yes: where, when, and what was registered?
- If no: was any aspect of the design registered after data collection but before analysis?
- If unknown: note as unknown
2. Did the published analysis match the pre-registered plan?
- Were primary outcomes changed?
- Were additional outcomes added post-hoc?
- Were subgroup analyses pre-specified or exploratory?
- Were exclusion criteria changed after data collection?
3. If not pre-registered: how many outcomes were tested in the study?
- How many are reported?
- What is the probability that at least one would reach p < .05 by chance given the number
  tested?

|-----------------------|--------------|---------------|-----------------|------------------|--------------|

SECTION C: EFFECT SIZE AND STATISTICAL POWER

1. What is the reported effect size? (r, d, OR, RR, eta2, etc.)
2. How does this effect size compare to:
- The field's typical effect sizes for similar constructs?
- The minimum effect size that would be practically or clinically meaningful?
- Later corrected meta-analyses or larger independent replications?
3. What was the sample size?
4. What statistical power did the study have to detect the reported effect at the reported
   size?
- Was the sample size determined in advance by power calculation?
- If not, at what power level was the study operating?
5. Winner's curse assessment: studies that barely achieve p < .05 in underpowered designs
   systematically overestimate effect sizes. Is the reported effect size likely inflated?

|--------------------|-------------------|--------------------------------|-----------|---------------|-------------------|

SECTION D2: FORKING PATHS / ANALYTICAL-CHOICE MULTIVERSE

P-hacking (Section B) and HARKing (Section D) ask whether the authors exploited flexibility.
This section asks a different question: independent of intent, HOW SENSITIVE is the reported
result to defensible analytical choices? A result that appears only under one specific
pipeline, and vanishes or reverses under equally reasonable alternatives, is fragile
regardless of whether the choice was made in good faith.

Do NOT re-evaluate measurement quality or confounding here; the construct-validity audit
handles that. Do NOT re-evaluate downstream citation use here; the citation-network audit
handles that. This section is strictly about the sensitivity of THIS finding to THIS study's
analytical decision points.

1. Map the analytical decision points actually made in the study:
   - Inclusion and exclusion criteria
   - Variable operationalization and coding thresholds
   - Model choice, covariate set, transformation, and outlier handling
   - Which comparison or contrast was treated as primary
   - Handling of missing or contradictory data For each, note whether it was pre-specified or
     appears to have emerged during analysis. Cross-reference Section B.

2. For each major decision point, generate the plausible alternative choices a competent
   analyst could have defensibly made instead.

3. Assess result sensitivity under those alternatives:
   - Would the headline effect HOLD, WEAKEN, DISAPPEAR, or REVERSE?
   - Is the result robust across most defensible branches, or does it live in one narrow
     corner of the analytical multiverse?
   - Where a multiverse or specification-curve analysis exists, report what it showed. Where
     none exists, state that robustness is UNTESTED, not confirmed.

4. Distinguish the cases explicitly:
   - ROBUST: the effect survives most reasonable specifications; analytical fragility is not
     the problem.
   - MIXED: the effect survives some defensible specifications but changes materially in
     magnitude, precision, subgroup, or interpretation.
   - FRAGILE: the effect depends on a specific pipeline and weakens, disappears, or reverses
     under defensible alternatives.
   - UNTESTED: alternatives have not been run and the available reporting cannot establish
     robustness.
   - CANNOT ASSESS: the analytical pipeline is reported too incompletely to map.

Required table:

| Decision point | Choice made | Pre-specified? | Defensible alternatives | Result under alternatives (holds / weakens / disappears / reverses) | Fragility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Fragility options: Robust / Mixed / Fragile / Untested / Cannot assess

Integration rule: FRAGILE results compound with p-hacking and HARKing. If a result is fragile,
shows p-hacking indicators, and was not pre-registered, do not count these as three
independent minor findings. Consolidate the overlap and evaluate the combined inference: the
reported result may be specific to the selected pipeline rather than a stable effect.

SECTION H2: THE CONCEDE TEST

Run this on every proposed finding before choosing the Section I verdict or rating the finding
in the final table.

For each finding, ask:

IF THIS FINDING WERE FULLY CONCEDED  -  granted entirely, with no argument  -  WOULD THE CLAIM
UNDER AUDIT STILL STAND?

- SURVIVES: the claim still holds. This is a limitation, not a defeater. Report it, but it may
  not be rated MAJOR or FATAL and may not anchor the verdict.
- NARROWS: the claim survives only in smaller scope, such as a smaller population, weaker or
  less stable effect, narrower conditions, or reduced downstream use. State the surviving
  claim explicitly.
- DEFEATS: the audited claim does not follow or the foundational finding is not sufficiently
  supported. This is load-bearing.

Create a table:

| Finding | Concede test: SURVIVES / NARROWS / DEFEATS | Surviving claim if NARROWS |
|---|---|---|

Do not turn a raw count of overlapping limitations into an invalidity verdict. Consolidate
duplicated findings and judge what scope the claim actually retains.

SCOPE-LOSS ASSESSMENT  -  REQUIRED FOR EVERY NARROWS RESULT

- INCIDENTAL NARROWING: precision or boundary conditions change, but the finding remains
  substantially the same in population, direction, magnitude class, and practical use. This
  ordinarily supports verdict B rather than C.
- MATERIAL NARROWING: the finding survives only as a meaningfully smaller, weaker, less
  stable, or less general result. The stronger use fails, but a useful narrower finding
  remains. This ordinarily supports verdict C.
- NEAR-DEFEAT NARROWING: only a trivial, tautological, or practically different remainder
  survives. Do not use NARROWS to avoid DEFEATS. Reclassify as DEFEATS when the remainder no
  longer performs the work for which the finding is used.

For each NARROWS result, add:

| Finding | Scope-loss level | Original claim | Surviving claim | What practical or inferential work is lost? |
|---|---|---|---|---|

VERDICT MAPPING

- No DEFEATS and no MATERIAL NARROWING: use A or B according to the remaining limitations and
  evidentiary strength.
- One or more MATERIAL NARROWING findings and no DEFEATS: ordinarily use C.
- D is appropriate where multiple adequately evidenced vulnerabilities compound or where a
  near-defeat remains unresolved, but the evidence does not yet justify E.
- E requires at least one adequately verified DEFEATS finding, or a verified combination of
  failures that makes the result likely an analytic or data artifact.
- If evidence is insufficient to run the test reliably: use F.

A PROVISIONAL replication failure cannot create a DEFEATS result or drive verdict E. Do not
use the concede test to cut genuine limitations; report them and enforce the severity cap.

SECTION I: INTERNAL VALIDITY VERDICT

Do not return one letter when a DEFEATS finding is present. Output a split claim-space object:

- Claim under audit: VALID / USEFUL-FOR-NARROWER / INVALID
- DEFEATS finding(s), if any:
- Surviving residual: [the exact narrower empirical claim left standing] OR "none"
- Internal-validity grade: A / B / C / D / E / F

"None" is a REQUIRED, reachable output. A failed or artifact-driven finding may leave no
usable residual. Do not manufacture one merely because the format asks for narrowing.

Internal-validity grades:

A. Strong - pre-registered, adequately powered, independently replicated, effect stable, data
available, and data generation sound. B. Acceptable - minor design limitations, plausible
effect size, and no load-bearing instability. C. Weak - underpowered, unreplicated, possibly
inflated, or affected by moderate design or data-generation concerns. D. Suspect - multiple
internal-validity problems, serious fragility, contested replication, or compromised data
generation. E. Invalid - adequately verified replication, analysis, or data-generation failure
DEFEATS the foundational finding. F. Cannot assess - insufficient reporting or retrieval.

Derive every line from the Concede Test and verification state of the load-bearing evidence.
Do not average across dimensions. Do not let a PROVISIONAL failed replication create INVALID.

Then answer:

1. Is the foundational finding likely to be real?
2. If it is real, is its magnitude sufficient to support the downstream claim?
3. What is the best current estimate of the true effect size?
4. What pre-registration and replication evidence would upgrade the grade?
5. What decisive test has not been run?
6. For AI-assisted analysis, did the adversarial check surface failures the original analysis
   missed?
7. Is the finding fragile to defensible analytical choices?
8. Does any usable residual remain? If yes, state it exactly. If no, write "surviving
   residual: none."

SECTION J: INTEGRATION WITH CONSTRUCT VALIDITY AUDIT

After completing this audit, note:

1. If the finding is suspect or invalid (D/E), the construct-validity problem is secondary  -
   the foundational claim is doubly weak.
2. If the finding is acceptable or strong but construct validity fails, the study may be
   internally valid but externally overclaimed.
3. If both internal validity and construct validity fail, note the compounding failure: the
   study may not measure what it claims AND the finding may not be real.
4. If data generation is compromised, note this as a third compounding failure: the study may
   not measure what it claims, the finding may not be real, AND the data may not represent the
   population the claim is made about.

The decisive-test sentence should integrate all applicable failures:

"The foundational finding [is/is not] likely to be real based on current replication evidence,
AND the construct being measured [does/does not] validly represent the downstream claim, AND
the data generation process [does/does not] support generalization to the claimed population
-  meaning the policy/institutional deployment rests on [a real but overclaimed finding / an
unreliable finding being overclaimed / a finding from unrepresentative data being overclaimed
/ all three]."

REMINDER

This prompt audits the finding itself  -  whether the result is real and stable  -  before
asking what it proves.

A finding that replicates cleanly but is overclaimed has one problem. A finding built on
flawed data has a second problem. A finding that doesn't replicate has a third problem. A
finding that doesn't replicate AND is overclaimed AND is built on flawed data is triply
invalid.

The toolkit now covers all three.

You may begin with suspicion. You may not end with suspicion.

Start wherever the anomaly appears. Finish with receipts.

RULES

1. A finding missing either mandatory falsifiability field is not inspectable and may not be
   rated MAJOR or FATAL.
2. Severity must agree with verification state. A PROVISIONAL finding cannot be MAJOR or
   FATAL.
3. A SURVIVES finding cannot be MAJOR or FATAL and may not anchor the verdict.
4. A NARROWS finding must state the surviving claim and scope-loss level. An incidental
   narrowing does not by itself require verdict C.
5. Target claim must match field 6 of the shared Claim Map. Use MAP GAP rather than silently
   substituting another claim.
6. A replication or post-publication finding must preserve its verification state. A
   PROVISIONAL failed replication may not create verdict E.
7. If no findings of consequence were produced, say so plainly and submit an empty table with
   a one-line statement. A prosecutor that never returns "nothing of consequence here" is not
   measuring anything.
