CLAIM EXTRACTION / INFLATION MAP
================================
Shared pre-stage. Runs AFTER target selection, BEFORE the prosecutors.

References: shared_primitives.txt §1 (verification states), §3 (failure-location)

-----------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS STAGE EXISTS
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Three prosecutors are about to audit this target. Without a shared claim map they
will audit three different claims: construct-validity attacks the paper's own
operationalization, internal-validity attacks the study design, citation-network
attacks the downstream restatement — and the resulting critique reads as
incoherent because each stage was aiming at a different object.

This stage produces the single claim map all three receive. It also does the
thing that most often separates a challenge that lands from one that gets waved
off: it locates WHERE the inflation begins, so the critique attacks the layer
actually responsible rather than the easiest target.

This stage does NOT evaluate whether the claim is true. It extracts and maps.
Judgment belongs to the prosecutors and the human. Resist the pull to start
critiquing here — a map contaminated by a verdict is worse than no map, because
every downstream stage inherits the verdict as a premise.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
INPUTS
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Target document:
  [Paste abstract, methods, results, conclusion. For non-academic targets: the
  full article / policy text / report / model response.]

Document type:
  [academic paper / review / policy document / journalism / institutional report /
   clinical guideline / dataset or statistical claim / AI model response / other]

Downstream uses, if available:
  [Paste how later papers, assessment reports, press releases, media, policy
  documents, or AI summaries describe this source. If none available, say so —
  do not invent them.]

The claim that prompted this audit:
  [What did you hear or read that made you want to check this? State it as you
  encountered it, in the form you encountered it. This is often the INFLATED
  version, which is exactly why it goes here — the map's job is partly to
  determine whether the thing you heard is what the source actually said.]

-----------------------------------------------------------------
OUTPUT — THE CLAIM MAP (six fields)
-----------------------------------------------------------------

1. WHAT WAS LITERALLY MEASURED

   The actual operationalization. Instrument, data source, population, sample,
   time period, unit of analysis. Be concrete and specific: not "gaming
   performance" but "character advancement rate in two MMOs, 2006-2008, among
   registered players, controlling for hours played."

   For non-academic targets: what is the underlying data or observation? A policy
   document's "outcome" is often an administrative metric; a news article's
   "study shows" points to a specific measurement. Name it.

   If the source does not clearly state what was measured, say so — that is
   itself a finding and it belongs in the map.

2. STRONGEST CLAIM THE SOURCE ITSELF MAKES

   Quote or closely paraphrase the source's own strongest assertion, PRESERVING
   ITS OWN HEDGING. If it says "suggests," keep "suggests." If it says "likely,"
   keep "likely." Note where this claim appears — abstract, conclusion, press
   quote — since abstracts routinely overstate the body.

   Sources frequently hedge properly in the methods and overstate in the
   abstract. Capture both if they differ.

3. WEAKER DEFENSIBLE CLAIM THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY SUPPORTS

   The narrower statement the measurement can carry. This is a reconstruction,
   not a quotation — state what the data would support with appropriate scope
   limits (population sampled, conditions tested, effect size, level of analysis).

   This field is the reference point for the entire audit. The gap between #2 and
   #3 is the paper-level overclaim; the gap between #3 and #4 is the downstream
   inflation. Both matter and they are different problems with different remedies.

4. BROADER CLAIM ATTACHED DOWNSTREAM

   What later citations, assessment reports, press releases, media, policy, or AI
   summaries assert on the basis of this source. Quote where possible.

   Label each downstream restatement with its layer (see primitives §3):
   citing literature / PR-media / policy / model-retrieval.

   If you have no downstream material, write "no downstream uses supplied —
   inflation analysis limited to the source itself" and mark fields 5 and 6
   PROVISIONAL. Do not reconstruct downstream claims from memory; a fabricated
   inflation chain corrupts every stage that follows.

5. WHERE THE INFLATION BEGINS

   The specific transition where the claim exceeds what the measurement supports.
   Name the passage, the citing paper, the press release sentence, or the
   summary. Be precise about the move: "MMO progression rate" → "gaming
   performance" is a construct substitution; "no difference in the sampled
   population" → "no difference" is a scope removal; "associated with" →
   "causes" is an inferential upgrade.

   Then classify the failure location using primitives §3:
     PAPER — the source itself makes the leap
     CITATION — later literature makes it
     PR / MEDIA — communications layer makes it
     MODEL / RETRIEVAL — summarizers reproduce the inflated version
     MIXED — specify which layer contributes which part

   State the verification state (primitives §1) of the evidence for this
   classification. If the downstream chain was not retrieved, this is PROVISIONAL.

   IMPORTANT: it is a legitimate and common finding that the source is narrow and
   defensible while the downstream layer is not. Do not force a PAPER
   classification. Attacking a careful paper for its press release is a category
   error and the fastest way for a critique to be dismissed.

6. WHICH CLAIM IS THIS AUDIT EVALUATING?

   State it in one sentence. Every prosecutor targets THIS claim.

   Choose deliberately and say why:
     - Auditing the SOURCE's claim (#2) tests whether the paper overreached.
     - Auditing the DEFENSIBLE claim (#3) tests whether even the narrow version
       holds.
     - Auditing the DOWNSTREAM claim (#4) tests the citation/PR/model layer, and
       the finding will be about that layer, NOT about the paper.

   If the audit targets #4, state explicitly: "This audit evaluates the
   downstream restatement. Findings about it are not findings about the source
   document." That one line prevents the most common and most damaging category
   error in the whole sequence.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
REQUIRED OUTPUT TABLE
-----------------------------------------------------------------

| Field | Content | Verification state |
|-------|---------|--------------------|
| 1. Literally measured | | |
| 2. Source's strongest claim | | |
| 3. Weaker defensible claim | | |
| 4. Downstream claim | | |
| 5. Inflation point + failure location | | |
| 6. Claim under audit | | |

Then state, in one line each:

  CLAIM UNDER AUDIT: [one sentence]
  FAILURE LOCATION (provisional): [paper / citation / PR-media / model / mixed]
  GAP TYPE: [source overclaims / downstream inflates / both / neither apparent]
  MAP CONFIDENCE: [high / moderate / low] + what would raise it

-----------------------------------------------------------------
RULES
-----------------------------------------------------------------

  1. EXTRACT, DO NOT EVALUATE. No severity ratings, no verdicts, no critique.
     The prosecutors do that. A map that arrives pre-judged makes every
     downstream stage a confirmation exercise.

  2. "NEITHER APPARENT" IS A VALID OUTPUT. If the source's claim matches its
     measurement and the downstream restatement is faithful, say so plainly. A
     claim map that always finds inflation is not a map, it is a template. This
     output should sometimes read: "no meaningful gap identified between
     measurement, claim, and downstream use."

  3. PRESERVE THE SOURCE'S HEDGES exactly in field 2. Stripping them here
     manufactures an overclaim that the source did not make, and every prosecutor
     will then audit a strawman.

  4. DO NOT INVENT DOWNSTREAM USES. If none were supplied, fields 4-5 are
     PROVISIONAL and the audit is scoped to the source alone.

  5. IF THE MAP IS AMBIGUOUS, SAY SO rather than resolving it. An honest "the
     source states X in the abstract and the more careful Y in the discussion,
     and which is 'the claim' is genuinely unclear" is more useful than a forced
     single answer — and it tells the prosecutors to audit both.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
HANDOFF
-----------------------------------------------------------------

This map goes to all three prosecutors unchanged. Each one:
  - audits the claim named in field 6
  - references the map's claim in the "Target claim" column of its finding table
    (primitives §2)
  - flags rather than silently substitutes if it finds itself auditing a claim
    not on this map — that means the map is incomplete, which is information

The counter-audit later asks whether the prosecutors attacked the strongest
version of this claim or the easiest downstream exaggeration. This map is what
makes that question answerable.
