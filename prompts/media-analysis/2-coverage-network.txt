COVERAGE NETWORK & SOURCE-CHAIN AUDIT  -  MEDIA BRANCH
====================================================
Prosecutor stage. Document-type variant of the citation-network audit. Runs across MULTIPLE
pieces covering the SAME event or claim.

References: shared_primitives.txt Section 1 (verification states), Section 2 (finding table)
Companion to: framing_construction_audit_media.txt (single-piece construction)

-----------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS AUDITS AND WHY IT'S DIFFERENT
-----------------------------------------------------------------

The single-piece framing audit asks: is this construction proportionate? That is a judgment
the auditor has to assert.

This audit supplies a BASELINE. Across five outlets covering the same event, a verb choice
stops being the auditor's characterization and becomes an observable choice: three wrote "drew
a knife," one wrote "produced a knife," one wrote "allegedly had a knife." The variance is the
evidence.

Two questions this answers that a single piece cannot:

  1. SOURCE INDEPENDENCE. If all coverage traces to one press release or one wire story, "five
     outlets reported X" is not five confirmations. It is one source amplified five times.
     This is the non-independence problem from the academic citation-network audit, with wire
     copy in place of a shared proxy network.

  2. SHARED CORE vs. CONSTRUCTION. Facts every outlet reports are probably established.
     Material unique to one outlet is either a scoop or a choice. The divergence map separates
     them.

WHAT THIS AUDIT CANNOT DO  -  STATE THIS IN THE OUTPUT: It maps how a story propagated and
where framings differ. It does NOT establish which framing is correct. "Twelve outlets framed
it this way, three that way" is a DISTRIBUTION, not a verdict. Majority coverage is not
evidence of accuracy  - especially when the majority shares one source. Adjudicating the
underlying facts requires primary material, which is downstream of this audit and human.

COMPACT MEDIA PRIMITIVE
-----------------------------------------------------------------

[MEDIA-P1] CAUSAL-ROLE AXES + GATES

CANONICAL RULE. This compact copy and every local execution copy must move
together. Local sections may add examples, but may not rename, narrow, broaden,
or change the disposition of these axes and gates.

Six axes. For each, extract the assignment the TEXT makes. Leave the axis blank
when the text supplies no usable assignment and the absence itself is not the
object of analysis. When non-assignment is materially part of the construction,
record the applicable null state rather than silently leaving it blank.

Do not infer an assignment from the headline alone. An assignment explicitly
stated in a headline may be recorded as a headline assignment; do not infer an
unstated cause, blame, role, or motive from it.

Never infer cause, blame, motive, role, or status from names, appearance,
employer, or location.

EVENT CLASSIFICATION
  The noun or frame assigned to the event itself. The noun is a claim. Where the
  absence of classification is materially relevant, record UNCLASSIFIED.

CAUSE
  Why the event happened: stated, alleged, or inferred motive; the explanation
  supplied for the event; or whether the text assigns no cause. Where materially
  relevant, record NO CAUSE ASSIGNED.

ROLE-AND-STATUS
  Who is cast as agent, victim, perpetrator, beneficiary, cost-bearer,
  bystander, official, whistleblower, defendant, or complainant.

BLAME / RESPONSIBILITY
  Who or what is assigned fault, including whether responsibility is
  individualized, institutionalized, diffused, or left unassigned.

REMEDY
  Whether a fix or solution is offered, whose fix it is, and who is called to
  act. Where materially relevant, record NO REMEDY FRAMED.

LEADERSHIP / POLICY
  How leadership decisions or policy are characterized, including competent,
  failing, humane, authoritarian, negligent, necessary, or ineffective
  characterizations, whether stated directly, appositionally, or through a
  source.

EPISTEMIC LEVEL - CAUSE AND BLAME

Classify every CAUSE and BLAME assignment:

  DOCUMENTED
    Direct statement, verified communication, or established record.

  OFFICIALLY-ALLEGED
    Law enforcement, complaint, indictment, or institutional finding.

  SOURCE-INFERRED
    Witness, advocate, expert, or interested party's inference.

  OUTLET-INFERRED
    The article's own synthesis.

  UNKNOWN
    Evidence insufficient.

Test for silent upward movement: an alleged cause restated as documented, or a
source's inference appearing later in outlet voice. Apply the ladder separately
to CAUSE and BLAME.

VERB-AS-TRACE

A verb or descriptor is reportable as causal-role framing only when it MOVES an
assignment on one of the six axes.

Examples:

- "breach" assigns migrant agency against a defended boundary;
- "waved through" reassigns cause to an authority;
- "pour" or "surge" can classify the event as flood-like mass movement.

A lexical difference that moves no assignment is REGISTER variance. Record it
as register only; do not report it as framing.

This test is distinct from the causal-bridge test. VERB-AS-TRACE asks what
causal-role assignment the language makes. The causal-bridge test asks whether
the WHY of a sequence is present or removed.

BRANCH-SPECIFIC LAYERS

Single-piece branch:
  Apply the mirror and flip guard to these axes. Cause, blame, and victim-role
  assignments are especially prior-sensitive. The branch must be able to
  decline to find a causal-role failure.

Coverage branch:
  Add CHOSEN vs. INHERITED, TEXT-READ vs. HEADLINE-ONLY, and the genre
  not-firing condition. These determine whose framing is being observed and
  whether the sample can support a divergence finding.

SECTION B  -  SOURCE CHAIN  (highest-value section)
=================================================================

Trace where each piece's information originates.

| Outlet | Named sources | Primary docs cited | Original reporting? | Traces to |
|---|---|---|---|---|

COUNTING UNIT - STATE IT BEFORE GIVING ANY NUMBER.   [FAIL-MB-008][FAIL-MB-009]

"Independent source" is ambiguous and the ambiguity is not resolvable by judgment. Count at
BOTH levels below and report BOTH as DESCRIPTION. The amplification VERDICT is not carried
by either whole-coverage number - it is carried by the per-fact table further down. Do not
skip that table.

LEVEL 1 - REPORTING ORGANIZATIONS (descriptive; comparatively stable) How many distinct
organizations produced original reporting - sent a journalist, conducted an interview,
obtained a document, or made a direct observation? An outlet that only republishes or
synthesizes does not count.

LEVEL 2 - UPSTREAM INFORMATION ORIGINS (descriptive; granularity-sensitive) How many
distinct origins does the reported material trace back to? Report it, but DO NOT read the
Level-1-vs-Level-2 relationship as a finding. Level 1 counts only original reporters (often
few); Level 2 counts every upstream origin (often many), so Level 2 routinely EXCEEDS Level
1. That direction carries no meaning, and the whole-coverage count is not comparable
run-to-run - the same coverage is defensibly counted at very different granularities.

WHOLE-COVERAGE RATIO - DESCRIPTIVE ONLY. NOT AN AMPLIFICATION VERDICT. pieces / Level 2 is
granularity-sensitive and misleads. A ratio near or below 1 is NOT evidence of independence:
a story can trace every disputed number to one source while its total origin count looks
large. State the number if you wish; it may not support any corroboration or amplification
claim. The amplification finding comes from the next table only.

AMPLIFICATION IS JUDGED PER CONTESTED FACT - LOAD-BEARING STEP, DO NOT SKIP. Amplification
is a property of a FACT, not of the coverage as a whole. Build this table for every
load-bearing or contested fact - the arrival/scale totals, the casualty figures, the stated
cause, and any figure a headline turns on (pull them from Sections C and D9):

| Contested / load-bearing fact | Pieces asserting it | INDEPENDENT origins for THIS fact | Single-origin? |
|---|---|---|---|

RULES FOR THE TABLE:
- Count origins PER FACT. "One government = one origin" applies here: an arrival total
  carried by six outlets all citing one ministry is 6 pieces / 1 origin, however many
  origins the whole story contains.
- DO NOT POOL facts. Each fact gets its own row. A fact single-sourced to one origin may not
  be averaged against a well-sourced one.
- A fact at [many pieces / 1 origin] IS amplification. Label it so on its row, regardless of
  the whole-coverage ratio.
- Per-fact counts are granularity-stable - a single disputed fact traces to few origins no
  matter how finely the whole network is split. This is why the per-fact table is comparable
  across runs when the whole-coverage counts are not.

REQUIRED STATEMENT - use this form: "Across [P] pieces, the load-bearing facts trace to
these independent origins: [fact - pieces / origins; ...]. [N] of these facts are
single-origin; apparent cross-outlet agreement on them is AMPLIFICATION, not confirmation."

SOURCE-CHAIN STATUS  -  a SEPARATE axis from verification state (Do NOT use
RECEPTION-CONFIRMED here. In shared_primitives Section 1 that means a source's own later
reception was checked  -  replies, replications, corrections. Comparing an article to a
press release is source-chain verification, a different operation entirely.)

  CHAIN-CONFIRMED ... primary source material or wire copy was available and directly
  compared against the piece CHAIN-INFERRED .... chain inferred from attribution,
  publication timing, or textual similarity, without the upstream document in hand
  CHAIN-PROVISIONAL . upstream source cannot be established

VERIFICATION STATE OF THE EVIDENCE USED  -  media branch subset:

  LOCATED ..................... the item exists; content unverified
  FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED ......... you read the full source and it supports the claim
  CROSS-OUTLET-CORROBORATED ... the fact is supported by 2+ INDEPENDENT origins
                                across outlets; state the per-fact origin count
  CONTESTED ................... outlets report it incompatibly
  PROVISIONAL ................. asserted from memory, or source not retrieved

  RECEPTION-CONFIRMED IS NOT USED IN THIS BRANCH. In shared_primitives Section 1 it means a
  source's own scholarly reception was checked  -  replies, replications, corrigenda. News
  coverage does not have reception in that sense. If you reach for it, you want
  CROSS-OUTLET-CORROBORATED or CHAIN-CONFIRMED.

  CROSS-OUTLET-CORROBORATED MUST CARRY THE PER-FACT ORIGIN COUNT. A fact may be labeled
  CROSS-OUTLET-CORROBORATED only if it has 2+ INDEPENDENT origins on its own row in the
  per-fact table. A fact carried by many outlets but tracing to ONE origin is AMPLIFICATION
  - mark it PROVISIONAL or amplified, never corroborated, even when the whole-coverage
  origin count is high. State the per-fact origin count alongside every use of this label.

Apply this subset separately to the documents supporting a finding. The two axes combine  -
e.g. the police statement may be FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED while the outlet's dependence on it is
only CHAIN-INFERRED.

Report both when both apply.

SECTION C  -  SHARED FACTUAL CORE vs. UNIQUE MATERIAL
=================================================================

  1. SHARED CORE: facts reported by all or nearly all pieces. List them.
     For each shared-core fact, check its row in the per-fact table in Section B.
     If it is single-origin, it is the shared origin's account amplified, not
     independent corroboration, however large the whole-coverage origin count is.

  2. UNIQUE MATERIAL: facts appearing in only one or two pieces. For each:
     - which outlet
     - scoop (original reporting) or selection (chose to include what others had available and
       omitted)?
     - does it cut for or against the dominant framing?

  3. CONTRADICTIONS: where pieces report the same fact differently. Quote both. Note whether
     the contradiction is factual (incompatible claims) or definitional (same event, different
     terms).

| Fact | Outlets reporting | Outlets omitting | Scoop or selection | Cuts which way |
|---|---|---|---|---|

AVAILABILITY MATRIX  -  REQUIRED BEFORE CALLING ANYTHING "SELECTION"

Outlets publish at different times with different information. Comparing a later piece (more
available) against an earlier brief (less available) and calling the difference "selection" is
the network-branch version of the timing error.

| Fact / source material | Available when A published? | B? | C? | D? |
|---|---|---|---|---|

RULE: a fact may be called SELECTION only if it was AVAILABLE before that piece published and
the piece still omitted it. Otherwise it is a TIMING ARTIFACT and must be labeled so.

State availability basis for each: known (documented release time) / inferred (other coverage
carried it earlier) / unknown. Unknown availability caps the finding at PROVISIONAL.

THE KEY ASYMMETRY: a fact available to all outlets  -  present in the primary source or in
earlier coverage  -  that some include and some omit, where the pattern correlates with
framing. That is selection, and unlike a single-piece omission finding, it is demonstrable
rather than asserted.

D7  -  NARRATIVE-FLAG AND CONTEXT VARIANCE ACROSS OUTLETS
-----------------------------------------------------------------

| Attribute / context element | Outlet A | Outlet B | Outlet C | ... |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Compare across outlets, where each is materially relevant: demographic attributes;
affiliation, funding, and interest disclosures; credential labels ("expert" vs. "critic");
biography and individualization; which prior episode is invoked as precedent; setting and
atmospheric cues.

The [MEDIA-P1] causal-role axes - EVENT CLASSIFICATION, CAUSE, ROLE-AND-STATUS, BLAME /
RESPONSIBILITY, REMEDY, and LEADERSHIP / POLICY - are owned by D-ROLE.

D7's inheritance test, TEST THE SOURCE OF THE UNIFORMITY, remains the shared
primitive both sections use.

D7 retains individualization/categorization because it measures identity
granularity, not causal-role assignment. Where the same text bears on both,
cross-reference the second section and record each distinct effect once.

For each divergence:
  1. Was the attribute known when EACH outlet published? (Use the availability matrix  -  do
     not count an unconfirmed attribute as an omission.)
  2. Is the difference inclusion, omission, delay, quote-routing, or euphemism?
  3. Materially relevant, or merely inflammatory?
  4. Does one outlet individualize a party another categorizes?
  5. Does the variance correlate with headline, sequencing, or source choices?
  6. Could timing, privacy rules, confirmation standards, house style, or outlet policy
     explain it?

NETWORK-WIDE PATTERNS  -  RECLASSIFY, DO NOT AUTOMATICALLY DOWNGRADE. If every outlet treats
an attribute the same way, that establishes the outlet is not an OUTLIER. It does NOT
establish that the treatment is proportionate or non-misleading. Every outlet can inherit the
same official euphemism, wire framing, legal convention, or shared blind spot.

  DOWNGRADE: any claim that this outlet is distinctive or slanted relative to peers. DO NOT
  DOWNGRADE: the underlying construction finding itself. RECORD SEPARATELY: a network-wide
  convention finding, which is often the more interesting result.

TEST THE SOURCE OF THE UNIFORMITY - CANONICAL FIVE-WAY CLASSIFICATION

  INDEPENDENT-EDITORIAL-CONVERGENCE
    Several newsrooms reached the treatment separately.

  WIRE-INHERITANCE
    One wire story appears under multiple mastheads.

  COMMON-PRIMARY-SOURCE
    Outlets worked from the same release, briefing, document, or speaker.

  LEGAL/PROFESSIONAL-CONVENTION
    The treatment follows an identification, sub judice, privacy, or other
    professional convention.

  SHARED-BLIND-SPOT
    No outlet examined the material point.

Only INDEPENDENT-EDITORIAL-CONVERGENCE is evidence that the treatment was
independently chosen across outlets. The other four are non-independent for the
D-ROLE inheritance gate and leave the substantive question open.

ABSENCE CLUSTER: are the same material attributes OMITTED by outlets that otherwise share a
framing cluster? Shared omission can be more diagnostic than shared wording, especially where
those outlets used DIFFERENT source chains  -  independent omission is a stronger signal than
common-source omission. Distinguish the two explicitly.

STEP 4 - GENRE / INDEPENDENCE CONDITION
Mandatory not-firing check.

State the sample's independence condition.

If most relevant assignments are INHERITED, as in wire-dominated breaking
coverage, the causal-role axis is UNDER-DETERMINED by this sample. Report:

"roles largely inherited or otherwise non-independent; independent causal
framing minimal - this genre under-determines the causal-role axis"

Mark any surviving divergence finding PROVISIONAL.

This axis is more likely to become testable in opinion, editorial, feature, or
later multi-source coverage once the wire monopoly breaks.

The section MUST be able to return:

"axis under-determined - wrong genre"

Do not manufacture divergence from register variance merely to fill the section.

STEP 5 - FLIP TEST
Symmetry and abuse guard.

Cause, blame, and victim-role assignments are especially prior-sensitive. Before
reporting any divergence finding, ask:

Would you assign the identical finding if the outlets' political valence were
reversed?

Name the specific assigning text: outlet, sentence or phrase, and the assignment
it makes. Never write only "outlet X is biased."

A finding that cannot identify the assigning text, or that would not be flagged
with valence reversed, is downgraded or dropped.

STEP 6 - FINDING TABLE

Each surviving CHOSEN divergence receives its own row in the standardized
finding table.

Use finding type: causal-role-framing.

OWNERSHIP RULE:

- causal-role-framing owns divergence on a [MEDIA-P1] axis;
- framing-divergence is reserved for framing that does not consist of a
  [MEDIA-P1] assignment;
- do not emit the same divergence under both types;
- D7 owns identity granularity and source-of-uniformity classification;
- D-ROLE owns causal-role assignment.

Cross-reference D7 where needed. Do not create a second finding from the same
assignment.

Do not fold multiple axes into one generic "framing differs" note. CAUSE and
ROLE-AND-STATUS divergences are separate rows when independently evidenced.

READ-NOT-SCORED

This section produces finding-table rows for the human reader. It does not
produce letters or concede-test verdicts.

Convergence across independent CHOSEN assignments is the signal; a scored rubric
is not.

D9  -  BASELINE AND DENOMINATOR VARIANCE
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Where outlets cite figures for the same phenomenon:

| Figure | Outlet | Baseline / denominator | Raw or rate | Adjustment applied | Impression supported |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

  1. Do outlets use different comparison points for the same underlying data?
  2. Does the choice correlate with framing cluster?
  3. Does any outlet report the comparison basis, or do they present bare numbers?
  4. Would a common basis change the apparent disagreement between outlets?

This is often the cleanest quantitative divergence available and it is invisible without
cross-outlet comparison.

SECTION D2  -  CROSS-BRANCH RESOLUTION  (run if a single-piece audit preceded this)
=================================================================

The single-piece framing audit flags items it CANNOT determine from one article and names this
branch as the test. Those flags are the highest-value work this audit does, because they are
the only findings in the system that can be CONFIRMED OR FALSIFIED rather than merely rated.

If no single-piece audit preceded this, write "no prior single-piece audit" and skip.
Otherwise, list every item that audit marked UNDETERMINABLE, PROVISIONAL, or "would be
resolved by the coverage-network branch," and resolve what this sample can.

THE STANDARD RESOLUTION TARGETS  -  check each explicitly:

  1. SOURCE-INTERNAL ORDERING (single-piece F2 Step 5). The single-piece audit could not tell
     whether an institutional source led with framing material or the outlet foregrounded it.
     TEST: how did other outlets covering the same statement order it? If most led with the
     factual account, the audited outlet's ordering was a choice. If most led with the framing
     material, the source spoke that way and the finding collapses. Also check: does any
     outlet quote or link a transcript, recording, or written release that fixes the order?

  2. VERB AND DESCRIPTOR CHOICE. The single-piece audit can only observe that a word is milder
     or more vivid than alternatives it imagines. TEST: what words did other outlets use for
     the same action - and CRITICALLY, what word does the PRIMARY SOURCE use? An institution
     often issues both a written release and a spoken briefing, and they frequently differ. A
     verb that looks like editorial softening may be DOCUMENT DEPENDENCE: the outlet used the
     presser, another outlet used the written report. Then route the surviving difference
     through [MEDIA-P1]. If the wording changes no event-classification, cause,
     role-and-status, blame, remedy, or leadership / policy assignment, record REGISTER
     variance only. If it moves an assignment, use it as evidence for a causal-role-framing
     finding. Both branches now emit findings in the [MEDIA-P1] vocabulary, so D2 lines them
     up directly rather than translating between two axis sets. Resolution therefore works
     regardless of which branch was the entry point. If document dependence explains the
     wording, the single-piece finding is not outlet construction - it is a
     sourcing-thoroughness observation and must be reclassified. Say so plainly.

  3. AVAILABILITY OF A BALANCING VOICE. The single-piece audit cannot tell whether a
     counter-voice was unavailable, refused, or unsought. TEST: did any outlet in the sample
     obtain one? If several did, availability is established and the single-piece finding
     strengthens. If NONE did, the absence is network-wide and is weak evidence about any one
     outlet.

  4. HEADLINE CHOICES. The single-piece audit judges a headline against the
     body. TEST: what did every other headline include and omit? If EVERY outlet
     made the same omission, downgrade the claim that the audited outlet is
     distinctive or uniquely slanted. Do NOT automatically downgrade the
     underlying construction finding; resolve it as NETWORK-WIDE and classify
     the source of uniformity.

  5. OMISSION vs. CONVENTION. Any fact the single-piece audit flagged as omitted. TEST: who
     else omitted it? A fact omitted by every outlet, especially one sitting in a primary
     document all of them had, is a NETWORK-WIDE pattern. That is a different and often more
     interesting finding than an outlet- specific one  -  and it is not evidence of that
     outlet's lean.

Required table:

| Single-piece finding | What it flagged as undeterminable | What this sample shows | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|

RESOLUTION values  -  use exactly these: CONFIRMED ......... the sample supports the
single-piece finding; it strengthens FALSIFIED ......... the sample shows the finding was
wrong; it must be CUT RECLASSIFIED ...... real, but a different finding type than originally
rated NETWORK-WIDE ...... the behavior is shared across outlets. This establishes the outlet
is not an OUTLIER. It does NOT establish the treatment is proportionate  -  every outlet can
inherit the same official euphemism, wire framing, legal convention, or blind spot. DOWNGRADE
the claim that this outlet is distinctive. DO NOT DOWNGRADE the underlying construction
finding. RECORD the network-wide convention as its own finding, and state its
source using D7's exact values:
INDEPENDENT-EDITORIAL-CONVERGENCE / WIRE-INHERITANCE /
COMMON-PRIMARY-SOURCE / LEGAL/PROFESSIONAL-CONVENTION /
SHARED-BLIND-SPOT. Only INDEPENDENT-EDITORIAL-CONVERGENCE suggests the
convention was independently chosen; the other four are non-independent and
leave the substantive question open. STILL UNRESOLVED .. this sample
cannot settle it; state what would NO EVIDENCE ....... you did not retrieve material bearing
on this item at all

EVIDENCE FLOOR  -  MANDATORY. CONFIRMED, FALSIFIED, RECLASSIFIED, and NETWORK-WIDE each
require you to NAME THE SPECIFIC EVIDENCE in the resolution cell: which outlet, which
document, which passage. A resolution without named evidence is not a resolution.

  If you did not retrieve material bearing on the item, the ONLY permitted values are STILL
  UNRESOLVED or NO EVIDENCE.

  CONFIRMED IS THE MOST DANGEROUS VALUE HERE. It ratifies an upstream finding, and it is the
  value most easily returned by a run that simply did not look. Absence of contradicting
  evidence is NOT confirmation. If your basis for CONFIRMED is "nothing I saw contradicted
  it," the correct value is STILL UNRESOLVED.

  Worked contrast, from actual runs of this prompt on the same event: asked whether an outlet
  reordered a police chief's statement, one run returned CONFIRMED having obtained no primary
  source; another returned FALSIFIED citing a specific outlet's report that the chief opened
  his briefing that way; a third returned STILL UNRESOLVED, lacking that outlet. Only the
  second and third were defensible.

REPORT FALSIFICATIONS PROMINENTLY. A finding this branch kills is the most valuable output the
system produces  -  it is the only place a claim gets tested against evidence rather than
re-rated by judgment. Do not bury it. If the single-piece audit was wrong, say which finding,
why, and what the evidence was.

NOTE ON DIRECTION: this section will more often WEAKEN single-piece findings than strengthen
them, because shared behavior is common and outlet-specific behavior is rarer than a
single-article reading suggests. That asymmetry is expected and is not a sign the section is
malfunctioning. If it never weakens anything across multiple runs, it is not doing its job.

=================================================================
SECTION E  -  CLUSTERING AND CORRELATION
=================================================================

  1. Do framing choices CLUSTER? Do outlets choosing one headline verb also place complicating
     context late, quote the same category of voice, omit the same facts?

  2. Describe clusters BY THEIR CHOICES FIRST, not by political valence. ("Cluster 1 leads
     with scene, places official account after paragraph 5, quotes advocacy voices; Cluster 2
     leads with official account, quotes officials, omits reaction.") Note any correlation
     with lean afterward, as an observation.

  3. Is there an outlet that does NOT cluster  -  mixed choices? Name it. A tidy two-cluster
     map with no outliers is suspicious and often reflects sample selection (Section G) rather
     than the coverage.

  4. TEST NON-LEAN EXPLANATIONS BEFORE CONCLUDING LEAN. Does clustering correlate with
     publication time, wire dependence, brief-vs-full-report, local-vs-national, or deadline?
     Length and timing explain more variance than people expect. State which you tested and
     what you found.

=================================================================
SECTION E2  -  PRIOR-MATCH CHECK  (MANDATORY, run before the flip test)
=================================================================

PRIOR SOURCE  -  declare this FIRST, before restating any prediction.

  SUPPLIED ......... the operator stated a prior in the inputs. MODEL-GENERATED .. no prior
  was supplied and you formed an expectation yourself about how coverage of this story type
  typically behaves. NONE ............. no prior was supplied and you did not form one.

YOU MAY GENERATE A PRIOR. An expectation that wire services lean official-sourced, or that a
story type clusters by outlet format, is a hypothesis from domain knowledge, and testing
hypotheses is the work. Do not suppress it. Declare it.

WHAT THE LABEL CHANGES:

  SUPPLIED, full match  -  the operator's expectation was confirmed. This is a
  motivated-reasoning risk. Items 5 and 6 are REQUIRED.

  MODEL-GENERATED, full match  -  WEAKER evidence than a supplied match, because the
  expectation and the finding were produced in the same pass by the same reasoner. Note this
  explicitly. Items 5 and 6 are still required.

  NONE  -  skip items 1 through 6 and write "no prior; prior-match check skipped." Do not
  invent one retrospectively to have something to check.

Then proceed with the existing items 1-6.

  1. Restate your prior's PREDICTION in one sentence: what did it lead you to expect this
     comparison would show?
  2. State your actual PRIMARY FINDING in one sentence.
  3. DO THEY MATCH? [full match / partial / no match]
  4. If FULL MATCH: not disqualifying, but the flip test and non-lean explanations carry MORE
     weight, and items 5-6 are required.
  5. WHAT DID YOU EXPECT AND NOT FIND? Name at least one thing your prior predicted that the
     coverage did NOT show. A prior confirmed in every particular is a prior that was not
     tested.
  6. INVERSE CHECK: the single strongest piece of evidence in this coverage AGAINST your prior
     -  not against your findings, against the prior itself.

=================================================================
SECTION F  -  FLIP TEST  (MANDATORY  -  DESIGNED TO BE COSTLY)
=================================================================

Answering "yes, I would flag the mirror" is free. This section requires producing something.

STEP 1  -  WRITE THE MIRROR, AS TEXT Take your #1 divergence finding. COMPOSE the actual
mirror  -  3-6 sentences of a headline plus lead written the way an outlet in the opposite
cluster would have written it, using the same technique you flagged. Not a description. The
text.

STEP 2  -  AUDIT YOUR OWN MIRROR Run your Section D criteria against what you just wrote.
Produce at least two findings against it with severity ratings. If you cannot produce findings
at comparable severity, your original finding is PRIOR-DEPENDENT  -  downgrade it.

STEP 3  -  CONSISTENCY CHECK Compare the PRINCIPLES you applied to your mirror against your
findings on the real coverage. Classify what you find  -  two different things, resolving
oppositely:

  (A) INCONSISTENT PRINCIPLE  -  you applied a rule to the mirror you did not apply to the
  original, or vice versa. One finding must give; state which and why. Mirror-side principles
  are less prior-contaminated (applied without knowing which side benefited), so they usually
  win.

  (B) SYMMETRIC PHENOMENON  -  the same construction lever appears in both, pushing opposite
  directions (e.g. either ordering constructs an impression). NO DOWNGRADE. This confirms the
  lever is a construction lever, which makes the outlet's specific choice more meaningful, not
  less.

  DO NOT DOWNGRADE BECAUSE THE MIRROR COMMITS THE MIRROR-IMAGE VERSION. That is the expected
  result and the reason the mirror exists  -  it tests whether you apply the same rule, not
  whether construction is universal.

| Observation | Type (A / B) | Resolution |
|---|---|---|

Downgrades are permitted here for TYPE A only.

STEP 4  -  FORCED RANKING Rank all findings from MOST prior-dependent to LEAST.

| Rank (1 = most prior-dependent) | Finding | Why more prior-dependent than the next |
|---|---|---|

STEP 5  -  AUTOMATIC DOWNGRADE  (not discretionary)

The finding ranked #1  -  MOST prior-dependent  -  IS DOWNGRADED ONE SEVERITY STEP.
Mechanical. No justification path, no opt-out.

  MAJOR -> MODERATE / MODERATE -> MINOR / MINOR -> cut or "reported, not load-bearing"

Earlier versions allowed declining the downgrade with a rebuttal. The rebuttal was always
supplied and the downgrade never happened. A check that can be argued away is not a check.

IF THE DOWNGRADE SEEMS WRONG, FIX THE RANKING IN STEP 4  -  you control it. Do not argue with
the consequence. Do not append a parenthetical rebutting it.

State in one line: "Downgrade applied: [finding] MODERATE -> MINOR." Report in Section I which
finding was downgraded and from what to what.

STEP 6  -  SCRUTINY SYMMETRY a. Point to a specific finding against an outlet you'd tend to
trust. If you have none, state that  -  it is a result. b. Where does the coverage cut AGAINST
what you expected? List anything. c. Name an outlet whose construction you would have flagged
had it leaned the other way. d. For counter-evidence you listed: "would a reader who noticed
it reach a different conclusion? If no, WHAT DOES THE COUNTER-EVIDENCE FAIL TO ADDRESS?" Name
what is missing from it. Do not restate what the coverage does  -  that is circular. The
phrase "noted but does not offset" is BANNED; reasoning that reduces to it downgrades the
affected findings one step.

| Finding | Mirror written? | Findings against mirror | Contradiction w/ mirror principle? | Prior-dependence rank | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Findings failing the flip test are PRIOR-DEPENDENT  -  downgrade or cut, labeled.

SECTION G  -  SAMPLE AUDIT  (audits your own selection)
=================================================================

These findings are only as good as the sample.

  1. How were pieces selected? Systematic or convenience?
  2. What outlets covering this event are ABSENT? Name them.
  3. Does the sample over-represent one end of the spectrum?
  4. Would adding the missing outlets likely change the clustering?
  5. Sample verdict: SYSTEMATIC / PARTIAL / CONVENIENCE

A CONVENIENCE sample can still show real divergence  -  verb variance is verb variance
regardless of how the pieces were found. But it CANNOT support claims about overall
distribution ("most outlets framed it as X"). State which claims your sample supports and
which it does not.

REQUIRED: if the sample is CONVENIENCE, every distributional claim in this audit is
PROVISIONAL. Say so in the verdict.

=================================================================
SCOPE LIMITS  -  RESTATE IN OUTPUT
=================================================================

  1. Coverage distribution is not a truth verdict. Majority framing is not
     evidence of accuracy, particularly when a load-bearing fact is
     single-origin in the Section B per-fact table.
  2. One event does not establish an outlet's practice. That requires a corpus across events
     -  a different and larger audit.
  3. Divergence does not establish intent. Editorial choice is observable; motive is not.
  4. Wire dependence, length, and deadline explain substantial variance. Test them before
     attributing divergence to lean.
  5. A convenience sample supports findings about the pieces examined, not about coverage
     generally.
