COVERAGE NETWORK & SOURCE-CHAIN AUDIT — MEDIA BRANCH
====================================================
Prosecutor stage. Document-type variant of the citation-network audit.
Runs across MULTIPLE pieces covering the SAME event or claim.

References: shared_primitives.txt §1 (verification states), §2 (finding table)
Companion to: framing_construction_audit_media.txt (single-piece construction)

-----------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS AUDITS AND WHY IT'S DIFFERENT
-----------------------------------------------------------------

The single-piece framing audit asks: is this construction proportionate? That is
a judgment the auditor has to assert.

This audit supplies a BASELINE. Across five outlets covering the same event, a
verb choice stops being the auditor's characterization and becomes an observable
choice: three wrote "drew a knife," one wrote "produced a knife," one wrote
"allegedly had a knife." The variance is the evidence.

Two questions this answers that a single piece cannot:

  1. SOURCE INDEPENDENCE. If all coverage traces to one press release or one wire
     story, "five outlets reported X" is not five confirmations. It is one source
     amplified five times. This is the non-independence problem from the academic
     citation-network audit, with wire copy in place of a shared proxy network.

  2. SHARED CORE vs. CONSTRUCTION. Facts every outlet reports are probably
     established. Material unique to one outlet is either a scoop or a choice.
     The divergence map separates them.

WHAT THIS AUDIT CANNOT DO — STATE THIS IN THE OUTPUT:
It maps how a story propagated and where framings differ. It does NOT establish
which framing is correct. "Twelve outlets framed it this way, three that way" is
a DISTRIBUTION, not a verdict. Majority coverage is not evidence of accuracy —
especially when the majority shares one source. Adjudicating the underlying facts
requires primary material, which is downstream of this audit and human.

SECTION A — COVERAGE INVENTORY
=================================================================

| Outlet | Headline | Pub date/time | Last updated | Correction note? | Headline changed? | Social/homepage headline if different | Byline | Wire attribution | Word count | Lead image subject |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

VERSION NOTE: media pieces change after publication. Where a headline was revised
or a correction appended, compare against an archive capture if available. A
headline that was changed after publication — especially without a correction
note — is itself a finding. Where you cannot check, write UNKNOWN rather than
assuming the current version is the original.

Then note:
  1. Publication sequence — which came first? Which appeared later and could have
     drawn on the earlier ones?
  2. Which carry a wire credit (AP, Reuters, local wire) versus original
     reporting?
  3. Which are briefs versus full reports? Length constrains what construction is
     even possible — a 200-word brief cannot include balancing voices.

SECTION B — SOURCE CHAIN  (highest-value section)
=================================================================

Trace where each piece's information originates.

| Outlet | Named sources | Primary docs cited | Original reporting? | Traces to |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Then answer:

  1. How many pieces contain ORIGINAL reporting — a source or document not
     present in the others?
  2. How many are substantially derived from a single upstream source: a press
     release, a wire story, an earlier piece, one official statement?
  3. INDEPENDENCE COUNT: how many genuinely independent information sources
     underlie this coverage? State the number plainly.
  4. Does any piece cite another piece in the set? Map the internal edges — this
     is the media equivalent of an internal citation cluster.
  5. Where identical or near-identical phrasing appears across outlets, quote it
     side by side. Shared wording is strong evidence of a shared upstream source.

REQUIRED STATEMENT:
  "This coverage represents approximately [N] independent information sources
   across [M] published pieces."

If N is much smaller than M, state the consequence explicitly: apparent
corroboration across outlets is amplification, not confirmation.

SOURCE-CHAIN STATUS — a SEPARATE axis from verification state
(Do NOT use RECEPTION-CONFIRMED here. In shared_primitives §1 that means a
source's own later reception was checked — replies, replications, corrections.
Comparing an article to a press release is source-chain verification, a different
operation entirely.)

  CHAIN-CONFIRMED ... primary source material or wire copy was available and
                      directly compared against the piece
  CHAIN-INFERRED .... chain inferred from attribution, publication timing, or
                      textual similarity, without the upstream document in hand
  CHAIN-PROVISIONAL . upstream source cannot be established

VERIFICATION STATE OF THE EVIDENCE USED — media branch subset:

  LOCATED ..................... the item exists; content unverified
  FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED ......... you read the full source and it supports the claim
  CROSS-OUTLET-CORROBORATED ... the fact appears in 2+ outlets
  CONTESTED ................... outlets report it incompatibly
  PROVISIONAL ................. asserted from memory, or source not retrieved

  RECEPTION-CONFIRMED IS NOT USED IN THIS BRANCH. In shared_primitives §1 it means
  a source's own scholarly reception was checked — replies, replications,
  corrigenda. News coverage does not have reception in that sense. If you reach for
  it, you want CROSS-OUTLET-CORROBORATED or CHAIN-CONFIRMED.

  CROSS-OUTLET-CORROBORATED MUST CARRY THE INDEPENDENCE COUNT. At a low count
  (§B3), multiple outlets reporting a fact is AMPLIFICATION of one upstream source,
  not corroboration. State the count alongside every use of this label, or mark it
  PROVISIONAL.

Apply this subset separately to
the documents supporting a finding. The two axes combine — e.g. the police
statement may be FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED while the outlet's dependence on it is only
CHAIN-INFERRED.

Report both when both apply.

=================================================================
SECTION C — SHARED FACTUAL CORE vs. UNIQUE MATERIAL
=================================================================

  1. SHARED CORE: facts reported by all or nearly all pieces. List them.
     Caveat: if the independence count (B3) is low, the shared core may just be
     the shared source's account, not independent corroboration.

  2. UNIQUE MATERIAL: facts appearing in only one or two pieces. For each:
     - which outlet
     - scoop (original reporting) or selection (chose to include what others had
       available and omitted)?
     - does it cut for or against the dominant framing?

  3. CONTRADICTIONS: where pieces report the same fact differently. Quote both.
     Note whether the contradiction is factual (incompatible claims) or
     definitional (same event, different terms).

| Fact | Outlets reporting | Outlets omitting | Scoop or selection | Cuts which way |
|---|---|---|---|---|

AVAILABILITY MATRIX — REQUIRED BEFORE CALLING ANYTHING "SELECTION"

Outlets publish at different times with different information. Comparing a later
piece (more available) against an earlier brief (less available) and calling the
difference "selection" is the network-branch version of the timing error.

| Fact / source material | Available when A published? | B? | C? | D? |
|---|---|---|---|---|

RULE: a fact may be called SELECTION only if it was AVAILABLE before that piece
published and the piece still omitted it. Otherwise it is a TIMING ARTIFACT and
must be labeled so.

State availability basis for each: known (documented release time) / inferred
(other coverage carried it earlier) / unknown. Unknown availability caps the
finding at PROVISIONAL.

THE KEY ASYMMETRY: a fact available to all outlets — present in the primary
source or in earlier coverage — that some include and some omit, where the
pattern correlates with framing. That is selection, and unlike a single-piece
omission finding, it is demonstrable rather than asserted.

=================================================================
SECTION D — FRAMING DIVERGENCE MAP
=================================================================

Where the baseline pays off. For each dimension, line up every outlet.

  1. HEADLINE VERB AND AGENCY
     | Outlet | Headline | Grammatical agent | Active/passive |

  2. KEY ACTION DESCRIPTORS — pick the 3-5 contested actions and show every
     outlet's wording side by side.
     | Action | Outlet A | Outlet B | Outlet C | ... |

     This is the most useful output of the audit. Verb variance across outlets
     covering identical facts is the cleanest available evidence that word choice
     is a choice rather than a description.

  3. SEQUENCING — where does each outlet place the material that most complicates
     its own lead?
     | Outlet | Lead subject | ¶ where complicating context appears |

  4. SOURCE DISTRIBUTION
     | Outlet | Voices quoted | Positions represented | Balancing voice present? |

  5. ATTRIBUTED vs. ASSERTED
     | Fact | Outlets asserting | Outlets attributing | Attribution verb |

  6. HEADLINE CONTENT — which material facts reach each headline?
     | Fact | Appears in headline of |

D7 — NARRATIVE-FLAG AND CONTEXT VARIANCE ACROSS OUTLETS
-----------------------------------------------------------------

| Attribute / context element | Outlet A | Outlet B | Outlet C | ... |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Compare across outlets, where each is materially relevant:
  demographic attributes; affiliation, funding, and interest disclosures;
  credential labels ("expert" vs. "critic"); role/status assignment; EVENT
  CLASSIFICATION (which noun each outlet chose); motive or cause attribution;
  biography and individualization; which prior episode is invoked as precedent;
  setting and atmospheric cues.

For each divergence:
  1. Was the attribute known when EACH outlet published? (Use the availability
     matrix — do not count an unconfirmed attribute as an omission.)
  2. Is the difference inclusion, omission, delay, quote-routing, or euphemism?
  3. Materially relevant, or merely inflammatory?
  4. Does one outlet individualize a party another categorizes?
  5. Does the variance correlate with headline, sequencing, or source choices?
  6. Could timing, privacy rules, confirmation standards, house style, or outlet
     policy explain it?

NETWORK-WIDE PATTERNS — RECLASSIFY, DO NOT AUTOMATICALLY DOWNGRADE.
If every outlet treats an attribute the same way, that establishes the outlet is not
an OUTLIER. It does NOT establish that the treatment is proportionate or
non-misleading. Every outlet can inherit the same official euphemism, wire framing,
legal convention, or shared blind spot.

  DOWNGRADE: any claim that this outlet is distinctive or slanted relative to peers.
  DO NOT DOWNGRADE: the underlying construction finding itself.
  RECORD SEPARATELY: a network-wide convention finding, which is often the more
  interesting result.

TEST THE SOURCE OF THE UNIFORMITY — these are different findings:
  independent editorial convergence (several newsrooms reached it separately)
  wire inheritance (one wire story, many mastheads)
  common primary source (all worked from one release or briefing)
  legal or professional convention (identification norms, sub judice rules)
  shared blind spot (nobody looked)
Only the first is evidence the convention is considered. The rest are evidence it is
inherited, which leaves the substantive question open.

ABSENCE CLUSTER: are the same material attributes OMITTED by outlets that otherwise
share a framing cluster? Shared omission can be more diagnostic than shared wording,
especially where those outlets used DIFFERENT source chains — independent omission is
a stronger signal than common-source omission. Distinguish the two explicitly.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
D8 — SYNONYM VARIANCE vs. CAUSAL-CONTEXT VARIANCE
-----------------------------------------------------------------

| Event element | Outlet wordings | Synonym only? | Causal context added/removed | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|

WHAT THE NETWORK ADDS — stated precisely. A single-piece audit CAN establish
causal-context loss by comparing the article against the primary material it
demonstrably used. The network does something narrower and still valuable: it shows
the alternative wording was FEASIBLE AND COMMONLY SELECTED, so the shorter
formulation was not forced by the source.

CAVEAT BUILT IN: outlets repeating the same wire sentence do not independently
demonstrate anything. Before treating cross-outlet agreement as evidence, establish
how many INDEPENDENT source chains it represents (Section B3).

  1. Which outlets include the causal bridge and which omit it?
  2. Does inclusion correlate with sequencing, sourcing, or framing cluster?
  3. CRITICAL — CHECK THE PRIMARY SOURCE, IN BOTH FORMS. A wording difference may
     be DOCUMENT DEPENDENCE rather than editorial choice: institutions often issue
     a written release AND a spoken briefing with different language, and outlets
     inherit whichever they used. Before rating a verb difference as construction,
     establish which source document each outlet worked from. (Cross-reference
     Section D2 item 2.)
  4. Rank causal-context variance ABOVE synonym variance where it changes the
     reader's model of the sequence.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
D9 — BASELINE AND DENOMINATOR VARIANCE
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Where outlets cite figures for the same phenomenon:

| Figure | Outlet | Baseline / denominator | Raw or rate | Adjustment applied | Impression supported |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

  1. Do outlets use different comparison points for the same underlying data?
  2. Does the choice correlate with framing cluster?
  3. Does any outlet report the comparison basis, or do they present bare numbers?
  4. Would a common basis change the apparent disagreement between outlets?

This is often the cleanest quantitative divergence available and it is invisible
without cross-outlet comparison.

SECTION D2 — CROSS-BRANCH RESOLUTION  (run if a single-piece audit preceded this)
=================================================================

The single-piece framing audit flags items it CANNOT determine from one article and
names this branch as the test. Those flags are the highest-value work this audit
does, because they are the only findings in the system that can be CONFIRMED OR
FALSIFIED rather than merely rated.

If no single-piece audit preceded this, write "no prior single-piece audit" and skip.
Otherwise, list every item that audit marked UNDETERMINABLE, PROVISIONAL, or
"would be resolved by the coverage-network branch," and resolve what this sample can.

THE STANDARD RESOLUTION TARGETS — check each explicitly:

  1. SOURCE-INTERNAL ORDERING (single-piece Step 5). The single-piece audit could
     not tell whether an institutional source led with framing material or the
     outlet foregrounded it.
       TEST: how did other outlets covering the same statement order it? If most
       led with the factual account, the audited outlet's ordering was a choice.
       If most led with the framing material, the source spoke that way and the
       finding collapses.
       Also check: does any outlet quote or link a transcript, recording, or
       written release that fixes the order?

  2. VERB AND DESCRIPTOR CHOICE. The single-piece audit can only observe that a
     word is milder or more vivid than alternatives it imagines.
       TEST: what words did other outlets use for the same action — and CRITICALLY,
       what word does the PRIMARY SOURCE use? An institution often issues both a
       written release and a spoken briefing, and they frequently differ. A verb
       that looks like editorial softening may be DOCUMENT DEPENDENCE: the outlet
       used the presser, another outlet used the written report.
       If so, the single-piece finding is not construction — it is a sourcing-
       thoroughness observation, and must be reclassified. Say so plainly.

  3. AVAILABILITY OF A BALANCING VOICE. The single-piece audit cannot tell whether
     a counter-voice was unavailable, refused, or unsought.
       TEST: did any outlet in the sample obtain one? If several did, availability
       is established and the single-piece finding strengthens. If NONE did, the
       absence is network-wide and is weak evidence about any one outlet.

  4. HEADLINE CHOICES. The single-piece audit judges a headline against the body.
       TEST: what did every other headline include and omit? If EVERY outlet made
       the same omission, that is a shared convention, not an outlet's slant — and
       the single-piece finding must be downgraded accordingly.

  5. OMISSION vs. CONVENTION. Any fact the single-piece audit flagged as omitted.
       TEST: who else omitted it? A fact omitted by every outlet, especially one
       sitting in a primary document all of them had, is a NETWORK-WIDE pattern.
       That is a different and often more interesting finding than an outlet-
       specific one — and it is not evidence of that outlet's lean.

Required table:

| Single-piece finding | What it flagged as undeterminable | What this sample shows | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|

RESOLUTION values — use exactly these:
  CONFIRMED ......... the sample supports the single-piece finding; it strengthens
  FALSIFIED ......... the sample shows the finding was wrong; it must be CUT
  RECLASSIFIED ...... real, but a different finding type than originally rated
  NETWORK-WIDE ...... the behavior is shared across outlets. This establishes the
                      outlet is not an OUTLIER. It does NOT establish the treatment
                      is proportionate — every outlet can inherit the same official
                      euphemism, wire framing, legal convention, or blind spot.
                      DOWNGRADE the claim that this outlet is distinctive.
                      DO NOT DOWNGRADE the underlying construction finding.
                      RECORD the network-wide convention as its own finding, and
                      state its source: independent convergence / wire inheritance /
                      common primary source / legal convention / shared blind spot.
                      Only independent convergence suggests the convention is
                      considered; the rest mean it was inherited, which leaves the
                      substantive question open.
  STILL UNRESOLVED .. this sample cannot settle it; state what would
  NO EVIDENCE ....... you did not retrieve material bearing on this item at all

EVIDENCE FLOOR — MANDATORY. CONFIRMED, FALSIFIED, RECLASSIFIED, and NETWORK-WIDE
each require you to NAME THE SPECIFIC EVIDENCE in the resolution cell: which outlet,
which document, which passage. A resolution without named evidence is not a
resolution.

  If you did not retrieve material bearing on the item, the ONLY permitted values
  are STILL UNRESOLVED or NO EVIDENCE.

  CONFIRMED IS THE MOST DANGEROUS VALUE HERE. It ratifies an upstream finding, and
  it is the value most easily returned by a run that simply did not look. Absence of
  contradicting evidence is NOT confirmation. If your basis for CONFIRMED is "nothing
  I saw contradicted it," the correct value is STILL UNRESOLVED.

  Worked contrast, from actual runs of this prompt on the same event: asked whether
  an outlet reordered a police chief's statement, one run returned CONFIRMED having
  obtained no primary source; another returned FALSIFIED citing a specific outlet's
  report that the chief opened his briefing that way; a third returned STILL
  UNRESOLVED, lacking that outlet. Only the second and third were defensible.

REPORT FALSIFICATIONS PROMINENTLY. A finding this branch kills is the most valuable
output the system produces — it is the only place a claim gets tested against
evidence rather than re-rated by judgment. Do not bury it. If the single-piece audit
was wrong, say which finding, why, and what the evidence was.

NOTE ON DIRECTION: this section will more often WEAKEN single-piece findings than
strengthen them, because shared behavior is common and outlet-specific behavior is
rarer than a single-article reading suggests. That asymmetry is expected and is not
a sign the section is malfunctioning. If it never weakens anything across multiple
runs, it is not doing its job.

=================================================================
SECTION E — CLUSTERING AND CORRELATION
=================================================================

  1. Do framing choices CLUSTER? Do outlets choosing one headline verb also place
     complicating context late, quote the same category of voice, omit the same
     facts?

  2. Describe clusters BY THEIR CHOICES FIRST, not by political valence. ("Cluster
     1 leads with scene, places official account after ¶5, quotes advocacy voices;
     Cluster 2 leads with official account, quotes officials, omits reaction.")
     Note any correlation with lean afterward, as an observation.

  3. Is there an outlet that does NOT cluster — mixed choices? Name it. A tidy
     two-cluster map with no outliers is suspicious and often reflects sample
     selection (Section G) rather than the coverage.

  4. TEST NON-LEAN EXPLANATIONS BEFORE CONCLUDING LEAN. Does clustering correlate
     with publication time, wire dependence, brief-vs-full-report,
     local-vs-national, or deadline? Length and timing explain more variance than
     people expect. State which you tested and what you found.

SECTION E2 — PRIOR-MATCH CHECK  (MANDATORY, run before the flip test)
=================================================================

  1. Restate your prior's PREDICTION in one sentence: what did it lead you to
     expect this comparison would show?
  2. State your actual PRIMARY FINDING in one sentence.
  3. DO THEY MATCH? [full match / partial / no match]
  4. If FULL MATCH: not disqualifying, but the flip test and non-lean explanations
     carry MORE weight, and items 5-6 are required.
  5. WHAT DID YOU EXPECT AND NOT FIND? Name at least one thing your prior predicted
     that the coverage did NOT show. A prior confirmed in every particular is a
     prior that was not tested.
  6. INVERSE CHECK: the single strongest piece of evidence in this coverage AGAINST
     your prior — not against your findings, against the prior itself.

SECTION F — FLIP TEST  (MANDATORY — DESIGNED TO BE COSTLY)
=================================================================

Answering "yes, I would flag the mirror" is free. This section requires producing
something.

STEP 1 — WRITE THE MIRROR, AS TEXT
Take your #1 divergence finding. COMPOSE the actual mirror — 3-6 sentences of a
headline plus lead written the way an outlet in the opposite cluster would have
written it, using the same technique you flagged. Not a description. The text.

STEP 2 — AUDIT YOUR OWN MIRROR
Run your Section D criteria against what you just wrote. Produce at least two
findings against it with severity ratings. If you cannot produce findings at
comparable severity, your original finding is PRIOR-DEPENDENT — downgrade it.

STEP 3 — CONSISTENCY CHECK
Compare the PRINCIPLES you applied to your mirror against your findings on the real
coverage. Classify what you find — two different things, resolving oppositely:

  (A) INCONSISTENT PRINCIPLE — you applied a rule to the mirror you did not apply
      to the original, or vice versa. One finding must give; state which and why.
      Mirror-side principles are less prior-contaminated (applied without knowing
      which side benefited), so they usually win.

  (B) SYMMETRIC PHENOMENON — the same construction lever appears in both, pushing
      opposite directions (e.g. either ordering constructs an impression).
      NO DOWNGRADE. This confirms the lever is a construction lever, which makes
      the outlet's specific choice more meaningful, not less.

  DO NOT DOWNGRADE BECAUSE THE MIRROR COMMITS THE MIRROR-IMAGE VERSION. That is the
  expected result and the reason the mirror exists — it tests whether you apply the
  same rule, not whether construction is universal.

| Observation | Type (A / B) | Resolution |
|---|---|---|

Downgrades are permitted here for TYPE A only.

STEP 4 — FORCED RANKING
Rank all findings from MOST prior-dependent to LEAST.

| Rank (1 = most prior-dependent) | Finding | Why more prior-dependent than the next |
|---|---|---|

STEP 5 — AUTOMATIC DOWNGRADE  (not discretionary)

The finding ranked #1 — MOST prior-dependent — IS DOWNGRADED ONE SEVERITY STEP.
Mechanical. No justification path, no opt-out.

  MAJOR → MODERATE / MODERATE → MINOR / MINOR → cut or "reported, not load-bearing"

Earlier versions allowed declining the downgrade with a rebuttal. The rebuttal was
always supplied and the downgrade never happened. A check that can be argued away
is not a check.

IF THE DOWNGRADE SEEMS WRONG, FIX THE RANKING IN STEP 4 — you control it. Do not
argue with the consequence. Do not append a parenthetical rebutting it.

State in one line: "Downgrade applied: [finding] MODERATE → MINOR."
Report in Section I which finding was downgraded and from what to what.

STEP 6 — SCRUTINY SYMMETRY
  a. Point to a specific finding against an outlet you'd tend to trust. If you have
     none, state that — it is a result.
  b. Where does the coverage cut AGAINST what you expected? List anything.
  c. Name an outlet whose construction you would have flagged had it leaned the
     other way.
  d. For counter-evidence you listed: "would a reader who noticed it reach a
     different conclusion? If no, WHAT DOES THE COUNTER-EVIDENCE FAIL TO ADDRESS?"
     Name what is missing from it. Do not restate what the coverage does — that is
     circular. The phrase "noted but does not offset" is BANNED; reasoning that
     reduces to it downgrades the affected findings one step.

| Finding | Mirror written? | Findings against mirror | Contradiction w/ mirror principle? | Prior-dependence rank | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Findings failing the flip test are PRIOR-DEPENDENT — downgrade or cut, labeled.

SECTION G — SAMPLE AUDIT  (audits your own selection)
=================================================================

These findings are only as good as the sample.

  1. How were pieces selected? Systematic or convenience?
  2. What outlets covering this event are ABSENT? Name them.
  3. Does the sample over-represent one end of the spectrum?
  4. Would adding the missing outlets likely change the clustering?
  5. Sample verdict: SYSTEMATIC / PARTIAL / CONVENIENCE

A CONVENIENCE sample can still show real divergence — verb variance is verb
variance regardless of how the pieces were found. But it CANNOT support claims
about overall distribution ("most outlets framed it as X"). State which claims
your sample supports and which it does not.

REQUIRED: if the sample is CONVENIENCE, every distributional claim in this audit
is PROVISIONAL. Say so in the verdict.

SECTION I — VERDICT
=================================================================

RETRIEVAL DEPTH — REPORT BEFORE THE VERDICT

Audit quality varies enormously with how much material was actually fetched, and
that variation is invisible in the output unless stated. A thin run and a deep run
produce equally confident-looking tables. Disclose:

  - Pieces read FULL TEXT: [n, list]
  - Pieces read SNIPPET ONLY: [n, list]
  - Pieces NOT retrieved but referenced: [n, list]

  - Searches or fetches performed: [approximate count]
  - Material you looked for and could NOT obtain: [list]

be load-bearing. State which findings this affects.

OR

The second must be genuinely available.

=================================================================
SCOPE LIMITS — RESTATE IN OUTPUT
=================================================================

  1. Coverage distribution is not a truth verdict. Majority framing is not
     evidence of accuracy, particularly at low independence counts.
  2. One event does not establish an outlet's practice. That requires a corpus
     across events — a different and larger audit.
  3. Divergence does not establish intent. Editorial choice is observable; motive
     is not.
  4. Wire dependence, length, and deadline explain substantial variance. Test
     them before attributing divergence to lean.
  5. A convenience sample supports findings about the pieces examined, not about
     coverage generally.
